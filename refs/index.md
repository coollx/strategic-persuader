| Strategic-Persuasion | *Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper.pdf)) — Cheng & You, ICLR 2026, [arXiv:2509.22989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22989) | method | ❌ no public code (searched 2026-08-11; `chengzr01/persuasion-gym` is a different paper) | Grounds LLM persuasion evaluation in Bayesian persuasion theory: repurposes four human-persuasion datasets (Anthropic, CMV, DDO, Perspectrum) into Sender-vs-Receiver opinion-change games where both sides are LLMs, measures persuasion gains on a 7-point stance scale, and validates the LLM Receiver proxy with a 45-participant human study. | Negotiation-Breakdown | *Dialogue Act-based Breakdown Detection in Negotiation Dialogues* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper.pdf)) — Yamaguchi, Iwasa & Fujita, EACL 2021, [ACL Anthology 2021.eacl-main.63](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.63/) | method | ✅ [repo/](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/repo) · [src](https://github.com/gucci-j/negotiation-breakdown-detection) | Introduces the JobInterview corpus — 2,639 crowd-sourced human-human negotiations over 5 job-contract issues with private preference profiles, one interdependent issue pair, and an exact utility function (92.9% agreement, only 4.9% breakdowns) — plus a breakdown detector that classifies regex-extracted dialogue-act sequences with a GRU, beating text models precisely where breakdowns are rare (average precision .418 vs .172 for BERT on JobInterview). The repo git-tracks the full dataset (`data.zip`, 3,935 records → the paper's 2,639 dialogues), a runnable utility model with a preference-profile generator and Pareto-frontier sweep (a ready-made negotiation environment or reward), the 6-regex + flow-automaton act extractor, and the React/Node collection interface; the paper's text-based baselines are absent, and the released trainer has two real bugs (gradient clipping after the optimizer step, inert attention mask). | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md) · [repo_analysis.md](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/repo_analysis.md) |
| GTAlign | *GTAlign: Game-Theoretic Alignment of LLM Assistants for Social Welfare* ([paper.pdf](related-work/GTAlign/paper.pdf)) — Zhu, Zhang, Cisneros-Velarde & You, arXiv preprint, [arXiv:2510.08872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08872), [OpenReview: ICLR 2026 rejected](https://openreview.net/forum?id=TWIlQMevJx) | method | ✅ [repo/](related-work/GTAlign/repo) · [src](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/GTAlign) | Trains Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (PPO on verl, Qwen3-32B judge served by SGLang) to emit a four-block reasoning chain — thinking, a JSON payoff matrix over user question styles × LLM response strategies, a self-solved Pareto analysis, then the response — with reward equal to the geometric mean of user and LLM welfare, so neglecting either side collapses the reward; gains in answer quality, token efficiency, and welfare hold in- and out-of-domain, and editing the payoff matrix mid-generation steers behavior without retraining. Also shows RL directly on abstract games neither converges nor transfers. The repo is a vendored verl fork whose transferable core is an async LLM-judge reward manager (`verl/workers/reward_manager/gamellm.py`) plus per-dataset welfare reward modules; training is single-turn (dialogue history flattened into the prompt), and two verl modules missing from the published commit must be restored from upstream before it runs. | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) · [repo_analysis.md](related-work/GTAlign/repo_analysis.md) |
| Chatbot-Ads | *Ads in AI Chatbots? An Analysis of How Large Language Models Navigate Conflicts of Interest* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper.pdf)) — Wu, Liu, Li, Tsvetkov & Griffiths, arXiv preprint, [arXiv:2604.08525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08525), [OpenReview: ICLR 2026 workshop AFAA poster, borderline accept](https://openreview.net/forum?id=kioO6a0oHM) | empirical | ❌ no public code (paper has no link; web search 2026-08-12 finds none; all prompts/stimuli printed in Appendix A) | Crosses Grice's four cooperative-conversation maxims with FTC advertising regulation to define seven conflict-of-interest scenarios, then measures 23 LLMs (7 families) in a flight-booking setting where a soft system prompt asks them to prioritize sponsoring airlines whose flights cost ~2x more. 18 of 23 models recommend the sponsored option >50% of the time (Grok-4.1 Fast 83%, Claude 4.5 Opus 28%), advertise more to users who appear wealthy (64% vs 49%, a gap chain-of-thought widens), never state false product facts but conceal sponsorship at a mean rate of 0.65, and all but Claude 4.5 Opus recommend predatory payday-loan sponsors at ≥60%. A logistic decomposition into base propensity, user-utility weight, and company-utility weight (fit per model) is directly reusable for measuring a trained persuader's welfare trade-off. | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md) |
| Janus-Protocol | *Competitive, Cooperative, and Everything In-Between: The Janus Protocol for Strategy-Infused Language Models* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper.pdf)) — Gray, Peasley, Hafızoğlu & Sen, Research Square preprint [rs-9000725/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9000725/v1), under review at the Springer journal Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems since 2026-03-01 (no reviews returned as of 2026-08-12; no OpenReview page) | method | ✅ [repo/](related-work/Janus-Protocol/repo) · [src](https://github.com/zacharytgray/Single-Price-Point-Negotiation-Domain) — undeclared; identified by author + exact fingerprint (eq. 2, Table 1 defaults, 11 special tokens, 23-strategy registry); no weights or data published | A frozen Qwen2-7B gets LoRA adapters (rank 16) whose per-rank scaling comes from tiny per-layer MLPs reading a single scalar ρ ∈ [0,1] — the target settlement position inside the Zone of Possible Agreement — so one adapter yields a continuous dial from buyer- to seller-favorable bargaining; trained by behavior-cloning 10,000 script-vs-script price negotiations stamped with their terminal ρ, plus a gate-separation loss against ignoring ρ. Headline: 55.3% vs 21.0% mean utility over the base model, with a role-inverted control collapsing to −4.3% (the dial is causal). The code is the real find: a clean no-PEFT conditional-LoRA implementation, a 23-strategy scripted opponent ladder, and the outcome-indexed supervision pipeline — but the cross-check shows the evaluation is unreliable (baseline on a different serving stack with oracle anchoring, unconditional ACCEPT, aggregates over unequal opponent sets, λ_sep unrecorded and 0 by default), so reuse the mechanism, not the numbers. | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) · [repo_analysis.md](related-work/Janus-Protocol/repo_analysis.md) |
