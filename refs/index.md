| Strategic-Persuasion | *Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper.pdf)) — Cheng & You, ICLR 2026, [arXiv:2509.22989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22989) | method | ❌ no public code (searched 2026-08-11; `chengzr01/persuasion-gym` is a different paper) | Grounds LLM persuasion evaluation in Bayesian persuasion theory: repurposes four human-persuasion datasets (Anthropic, CMV, DDO, Perspectrum) into Sender-vs-Receiver opinion-change games where both sides are LLMs, measures persuasion gains on a 7-point stance scale, and validates the LLM Receiver proxy with a 45-participant human study. Frontier Senders gain most (DeepSeek-R1: 0.23 static / 1.27 dynamic), and RL (PPO/GRPO via verl) roughly doubles a Llama-3.2-3B Sender's dynamic gains with transfer across Receiver architectures. All prompts and hyperparameters are in the appendices, so the pipeline is reproducible despite no released code. | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md) |
| Negotiation-Breakdown | *Dialogue Act-based Breakdown Detection in Negotiation Dialogues* ([paper.pdf](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper.pdf)) — Yamaguchi, Iwasa & Fujita, EACL 2021, [ACL Anthology 2021.eacl-main.63](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.63/) | method | ✅ [repo/](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/repo) · [src](https://github.com/gucci-j/negotiation-breakdown-detection) | Introduces the JobInterview corpus — 2,639 crowd-sourced human-human negotiations over 5 job-contract issues with private preference profiles, one interdependent issue pair, and an exact utility function (92.9% agreement, only 4.9% breakdowns) — plus a breakdown detector that classifies regex-extracted dialogue-act sequences with a GRU, beating text models precisely where breakdowns are rare (average precision .418 vs .172 for BERT on JobInterview). The repo git-tracks the full dataset (`data.zip`, 3,935 records → the paper's 2,639 dialogues), a runnable utility model with a preference-profile generator and Pareto-frontier sweep (a ready-made negotiation environment or reward), the 6-regex + flow-automaton act extractor, and the React/Node collection interface; the paper's text-based baselines are absent, and the released trainer has two real bugs (gradient clipping after the optimizer step, inert attention mask). | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md) · [repo_analysis.md](related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/repo_analysis.md) |
| GTAlign | *GTAlign: Game-Theoretic Alignment of LLM Assistants for Social Welfare* ([paper.pdf](related-work/GTAlign/paper.pdf)) — Zhu, Zhang, Cisneros-Velarde & You, arXiv preprint, [arXiv:2510.08872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08872), [OpenReview: ICLR 2026 rejected](https://openreview.net/forum?id=TWIlQMevJx) | method | ✅ [repo/](related-work/GTAlign/repo) · [src](https://github.com/ulab-uiuc/GTAlign) | Trains Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (PPO on verl, Qwen3-32B judge served by SGLang) to emit a four-block reasoning chain — thinking, a JSON payoff matrix over user question styles × LLM response strategies, a self-solved Pareto analysis, then the response — with reward equal to the geometric mean of user and LLM welfare, so neglecting either side collapses the reward; gains in answer quality, token efficiency, and welfare hold in- and out-of-domain, and editing the payoff matrix mid-generation steers behavior without retraining. Also shows RL directly on abstract games neither converges nor transfers. The repo is a vendored verl fork whose transferable core is an async LLM-judge reward manager (`verl/workers/reward_manager/gamellm.py`) plus per-dataset welfare reward modules; training is single-turn (dialogue history flattened into the prompt), and two verl modules missing from the published commit must be restored from upstream before it runs. | [paper_analysis.md](related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) · [repo_analysis.md](related-work/GTAlign/repo_analysis.md) |
