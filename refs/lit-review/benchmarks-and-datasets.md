# Task families and datasets

**Question.** Which task family and which datasets support this project's claim — one reinforcement-learning-trained negotiator whose persuasion profile moves surplus share while efficiency stays high — and why were the alternatives rejected?

**Sources.** Distilled from `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-10/benchmarks.md` and `seed-papers.md`; `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/asymmetric-datasets.md`, `richer-datasets.md`, `advisor-landscape.md`, and `attributes-alignment.md`; `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-12/opinion-change-utilities.md`. Reference analyses linked where they bear: [Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md), [Strategic-Persuasion](../related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md), [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md), [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md), [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md).

## 1. Task families

**Verdict.** Multi-issue negotiation is the only family in which a welfare weight (λ) has two independent quantities to move. Every other family collapses to one number — either because there is only one party with a payoff, or because the two payoffs sum to a constant. The filter that selects it is three-part: both sides must have a numeric utility computed by arithmetic rather than by a judgment, each side's utility parameters must be private and drawn per episode, and the interaction must run over multiple free-language turns.

### 1.1 Five families, defined by who can veto the outcome

**Multi-issue negotiation.** Two parties allocate several issues between themselves; each scores the resulting package from its own private table of points. Both parties hold veto power — no allocation takes effect unless both accept — and each party's walk-away sets the price of using that veto.

**Single-issue negotiation.** Two parties agree on one number, almost always a price, with each holding a private reservation value. The veto structure is identical to multi-issue, but the outcome space is one-dimensional, so the two payoffs move against each other exactly.

**Advising under conflict of interest.** An advisor knows a catalog and its own private payment for steering the choice; the customer knows its own hidden needs and is the only party who can transact. The customer holds sole veto power — the advisor can only speak — and the advisor's payoff arrives from a third party rather than from the customer.

**Opinion change.** A sender argues a claim and a receiver reports a stance. The receiver holds sole veto power over its own stance, and there is nothing to allocate: the sender's payoff is read directly off the receiver's reported position.

**Behavioral persuasion.** A sender argues for an action that costs the receiver something real — a donation, a purchase — and the receiver takes it or does not. The receiver holds sole veto power over the action, and its payoff is what it keeps by refusing; the sender usually has no numeric payoff at all.

**Social-deduction games.** Several players talk and then vote; a team wins or loses. No single player holds veto power, the outcome is a team-level win or loss that is zero-sum across teams, and the hidden variable is an identity rather than a utility.

### 1.2 The filter, applied

Two-sided numeric utility rules out opinion change (only the receiver's stance is scored, and it *is* the sender's payoff), behavioral persuasion as instantiated in Persuasion for Good (arXiv 1906.06725) (the persuadee's donation is incentive-compatible real money, but the persuader had no stake, so there is only one number), and SOTOPIA (arXiv 2310.11667) and its family (a language-model judge scores seven social dimensions, and the SOTOPIA-RL paper's own contribution is redesigning that reward because episode-level judging is too coarse and invites reward hacking).

Hidden types rules out advising as currently published — [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md) (arXiv 2604.08525) is a set of one-shot prompted vignettes with no per-episode private customer parameter, and [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) (arXiv 2510.08872), the closest thing in the language-model literature to a two-sided continuous welfare reward, defines the user's welfare as a designer-chosen function of answer quality, length, and latency with aggregation weights fixed before training rather than sampled per episode — and it rules out CraigslistBargain (arXiv 1808.09637) as a primary environment, where the buyer's target price is private but the seller's anchor is the public listing price. Advising also carries a structural risk beyond data availability: with one product chosen from a catalog the frontier is a small staircase of discrete points, so a welfare weight may collapse to two behaviours (best fit, or highest payment the customer will still accept) with nothing interesting in between.

Multi-turn free language rules out the automated-negotiation domain libraries, which have the richest utility spaces in existence and no dialogue at all, and rules out [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md), whose model emits numeric offer tokens and never natural language.

### 1.3 Why opinion change cannot carry a welfare-weight claim

The receiver in opinion change has a stance, not a payoff. Across the whole family — Change My View (arXiv 1602.01103), the Anthropic persuasion dataset, debate.org (arXiv 1906.11310), Perspectrum (arXiv 1906.03538), Persuade Me If You Can (arXiv 2503.01829), DuET-PD (arXiv 2508.17450) — the outcome is a binary success flag, a Likert stance, or a stance delta, and in every case that quantity is what the sender is scored on. There is no second number saying whether the receiver was made better or worse off, so there is nothing for a welfare weight to weigh.

The one paper that writes a receiver utility down does not compute it. [Strategic-Persuasion](../related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md) (arXiv 2509.22989) sets up canonical Bayesian persuasion with utilities for both parties, but the receiver utility u is notation: it is never computed in any experiment and never enters the reward, and the receiver objective is a fixed truth-tracking loss identical across every instance. What varies across its instances is the claim, the prior, and the drawn state — never a receiver preference — so there is nothing about *this* receiver for a sender to infer, and its information asymmetry is over the world state rather than over preferences. Its reinforcement-learning reward is the sender's realized utility minus a sender-side prior baseline; the receiver's side never appears.

The receiver-side quantity that does exist in parts of this family is an accuracy, not a welfare: the Anthropic Likert delta, DuET-PD's multiple-choice correctness, and the debate lineage's judge accuracy all measure "did the receiver end up right." Mixing an accuracy into the sender's reward produces an honesty penalty, not a welfare trade-off. A welfare term is meaningful only when the receiver could rationally want something other than to be maximally accurate — which a private valuation gives and a truth-tracking loss does not. The debate lineage (arXiv 1805.00899 and successors) makes the same point from the other side: the judge has no utility function because judge accuracy is deliberately kept outside every debater's optimization target, and that refusal is the safety property.

The nearest miss in the whole family is GLEE (arXiv 2410.05254), whose persuasion games give both parties numeric utilities with the receiver's valuation varying per configuration and optionally hidden from the sender. It still fails: the receiver's action is binary per round so there is no margin to leave within a round, the sender's utility is binary and state-independent so it cannot want partial movement, and its efficiency and fairness numbers are post-hoc evaluation metrics that nobody has combined into a training reward.

### 1.4 Why pure single-issue cannot either, and how walk-away softens it

Conditional on a deal closing, single-issue bargaining has a constant total. With buyer surplus and seller surplus summing to a fixed quantity T, the per-episode reward λ · own + (1 − λ) · other equals (2λ − 1) · own plus a term that does not depend on the seller's choices. Maximizing that reward means maximizing own points for every λ above 0.5 and minimizing them for every λ below it. The dial has three settings, not a curve, and there is no efficiency axis at all: every deal that closes captures the entire available surplus by construction, so efficiency cannot be held high as a maintained axiom while the profile moves surplus share, because efficiency is not free to vary.

[Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) (Research Square rs-9000725) is the clean exhibit. Its domain is a zero-sum single-price bilateral negotiation, its control scalar is defined as the normalized settlement position inside the zone of possible agreement, and training is outcome-conditioned behavior cloning over scripted-strategy rollouts. The dial and the surplus share are literally the same number, so the design has nothing left to measure — and the paper's evaluation is in any case unreliable, worth reusing for its mechanism rather than its numbers.

The walk-away softens this, and it is the reason single-issue work is not vacuous. The deal-versus-no-deal margin is not part of the fixed pie: realized joint points are T when a deal closes and the sum of the two walk-aways when it does not, and the first exceeds the second whenever a zone of agreement exists. So λ still changes how hard a seller pushes before the buyer walks. Training Language Models for Bilateral Trade with Private Information (arXiv 2604.16472) measures exactly this pathology: supervised fine-tuning roughly doubles surplus share but reduces deal rate, and reinforcement learning then recovers deal rate while eroding the surplus gain, which the authors name as a reward-design flaw. That is close to a motivating negative result for an explicit welfare weight.

But the axis walk-away restores is binary and coarse, and it conflates the two things this project must keep apart: whether the pie was found and how it was split. Multi-issue structure restores a continuous efficiency axis because the joint total depends on *which* allocation is chosen, not only on whether one is chosen — which is what makes "efficiency stays high at every profile level" a checkable axiom rather than a tautology.

## 2. Dataset inventory

**Verdict.** CaSiNo (arXiv 2103.15721) is the environment. It is the only public asset that gives both sides a ground-truth numeric utility, hides each side's priority order from the other, produces genuinely persuasive human language, ships under a permissive license, and has a scenario generator small enough to reimplement as a live environment in an afternoon. Everything else is either a component to borrow or a rejected alternative.

### 2.1 CaSiNo — the chosen rules

Two participants play campsite neighbours negotiating three packages each of food, water, and firewood. Each side independently draws a random permutation of high, medium, and low priority over the three issues, worth 5, 4, and 3 points per package, so one side taking everything scores 36 points. Walking away pays both sides 5 points — a non-zero disagreement point, unlike Deal or No Deal's zero. Dialogue is free-form and alternating with a minimum of 10 utterances; the corpus averages 11.6 utterances of roughly 22 tokens each, which is an order of magnitude more language per dialogue than any comparable corpus. The released corpus is 1,030 dialogues from 846 participants, CC-BY-4.0, with a bonus of 8.33 cents per point paid immediately, so the human preferences were incentive-compatible.

The conflict-alignment level falls out of the two priority orders and takes exactly three values. Maximum joint points is three times the sum over issues of whichever side values that issue more. When the two orders are identical the ceiling is 36 and the pie is fixed — this is the near-zero-sum stratum. Of the six possible counterpart orders given one side's order, one produces 36, two produce 39, and three produce 42. That is the whole conflict-alignment structure, computable before the dialogue starts, and it maps onto CaSiNo's own integrative-potential levels 1 to 3. CaSiNo's human anchor for the efficiency axis is a correlation of 0.425 (p < 0.001) between integrative potential and realized joint points: people do find the joint gains, imperfectly.

The priority tables are ground truth by construction — assigned by the generator, displayed only to their owner, never inferred from text — which is what makes both the reward and the opponent estimate exact rather than annotated. The cost is coarseness: with three issues there are only six possible hidden orders, so scoring an opponent estimate is a six-way ranking problem rather than cardinal utility estimation.

The tactic annotations are a usable but partial instrument. The annotated subset covers 396 dialogues and 4,615 utterances labelled by three expert annotators with nine strategies: small talk, empathy, coordination, no-need, elicit-preference, undervalue-partner, vouch-fairness, self-need, and other-need, plus a non-strategic residual. **Two labels were dropped by the authors themselves** — empathy and coordination, both at Krippendorff's alpha 0.42 against 0.62 to 0.89 for the seven they kept — so the released multi-task classifier covers seven labels, not nine, at overall F1 68.3 and joint accuracy 70.2, with the two rarest kept labels (no-need at 46.2, undervalue-partner at 47.3) near coin-flip quality. Any panel built on these labels inherits that: the strategic labels are measurable in this instrument and the affective ones are not. Related assets on the same corpus: an emotion instrument validated on CaSiNo itself (arXiv 2107.13165), a theory-of-mind benchmark built directly on its dialogues (arXiv 2404.13627), an opponent-modeling line (arXiv 2205.00344), a tool-integrated agent with an offer optimizer (arXiv 2503.07129, Apache-2.0), and a Bayesian belief-distillation agent (arXiv 2605.04507) reporting calibration over the six possible priority orders.

The known weaknesses, stated plainly: the game is structurally symmetric — both sides divide the same three pools — so a seller-and-buyer surface is a costume rather than an asymmetry in the mathematics; and reward shaping over a selfishness-versus-fairness mix has already been run on this exact task (arXiv 2310.14404), whose central finding is that naive self-play over-represents agreement in its own training data and therefore never teaches the value of compromise, producing agents that walk away far too often against real humans.

### 2.2 The near misses

**Deal or No Deal (arXiv 1706.05125).** The ancestor: 5,808 human-human dialogues over 2,236 scenarios, both sides' value vectors private and summing to 10, walk-away worth zero, up to ten turns, with mismatched final reports scoring zero for both. Two facts carry forward. First, its reinforcement-learning partner is **frozen** — a fixed supervised imitator, not a co-adapting copy, and the authors froze it because updating both sides drove the language away from English; the paper's own "self-play" label is misleading on this point. Second, the resulting agent wins more points on the deals it closes (8.0 against its human partner's 7.1) while only 57.2 percent of its dialogues reach a deal, because it negotiates harder and humans walk away rather than capitulate. That is the strongest single empirical argument in the literature for a counterpart-utility term in the reward, and simultaneously a warning that a frozen counterpart will flatter us. Its weakness as an environment is that the items are semantically arbitrary, so dialogues degenerate into bare offer exchange.

**JobInterview ([Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md), EACL 2021).** The richest mechanics available. Recruiter versus worker over five issues — salary, weekly holiday, position, company, workplace — where the two integer issues are role-opposed inside the scoring function (the recruiter scores descending in salary, the worker ascending), so the asymmetry lives in the mathematics rather than in the labels, while the three categorical issues carry the integrative room. Position depends on company, giving a genuine non-linearity, and the outcome space is 9,920 possible agreements against Deal or No Deal's 22.5. The shipped data carries **both** negotiators' complete cardinal utility tables, so an opponent estimate could be scored against real-valued ground truth rather than a six-way ranking. Corpus: 3,935 records, 2,639 usable after dropping aborted ones, 2,500 completed, 7,870 utility profiles, MIT-licensed with the collection interface included. Its generator is extensible almost for free: the issue list is a 60-line declarative data file, adding an issue is one object literal, the weight rule is one expression, a second dependency is one field, and the scorer already dispatches on issue type and dependency without modification.

The disqualifier is language. A median dialogue is 63 words across 11 messages, mean 6.24 words per message — chat fragments and banter, not argument, with only a quarter of dialogues reaching 100 words. Against CaSiNo's roughly 255 words of reasoned appeal per dialogue, JobInterview teaches terseness. The utility machinery is what we want and the prose is not, and the prose is what a persuader has to imitate.

**CraigslistBargain (arXiv 1808.09637).** 6,682 human-human dialogues over real listings in six categories, with side offers and cheap talk explicitly encouraged — multi-issue *talk* on a single-issue *ledger*, since free delivery and bundled accessories have no price in the utility function. The buyer's target is private at 0.5, 0.7, or 0.9 times the listing price; the seller's target is the public listing price, so only one side's preference is hidden. MIT-licensed with a live environment framework. Its role here is exactly two things: language register for the supervised stage, and real listings as scenario seeds for a later sales instantiation.

**ANAC / GENIUS domains via NegMAS.** 180 domain files parsing to 51 distinct domains by issue-name signature, spanning 1 to 50 issues (median 5) and outcome spaces from 1 to 1,944,000 (median 320), all linear-additive in the shipped set, BSD 3-Clause, with a programmatic generator that lets you specify the shape of the frontier directly — zero-sum, piecewise-linear, or curved. Zero dialogues. No published work has put a language-model dialogue layer on these domains, which is a real and defensible gap; it is also not this project's gap. Hold it as an optional utility-space source if the conflict-alignment structure ever needs to be varied more finely than three levels.

**AmazonHistoryPrice (arXiv 2402.15813).** 930 real products across 18 categories with real price histories, formalized as an asymmetric incomplete-information game, Apache-2.0. Buyers systematically underperform sellers, and their fix — a deterministic offer generator separated from a language narrator — lifts buyer deal rate from 26.67 percent to 88.88 percent. That decomposition, numeric decision separated from language surface, recurs in CraigslistBargain's coarse dialogue acts and in the bilateral-trade paper's tool-call offers. Rejected as a primary environment for the same reason as CraigslistBargain: the seller side is anchored to a public list price.

**Bilateral trade (arXiv 2604.16472).** The cleanest closed-form two-sided utility found anywhere: buyer surplus and seller surplus from a single price, negative-infinity for individual-rationality violations, both reservation prices private, and — uniquely — neither party knows whether a zone of agreement exists at all, since scenarios include cases where the rational outcome is no deal. Three separated evaluation dimensions: individual rationality, surplus share, and pair-level deal rate. Its reward-design finding is quoted in section 1.4 above and is the strongest external evidence that the own-versus-counterpart trade-off needs an explicit knob. Single-issue, and code availability is unconfirmed.

**LLM-Deliberation (arXiv 2309.17234).** MIT-licensed, exact integer score sheets per party with a private minimum threshold, tunable difficulty, adversarial roles, base game six agents and five issues. Only five or six hand-authored games ship and there is no human dialogue, so it is a fallback rather than a primary: multi-party credit assignment is complexity this project does not need, and the sender-versus-receiver asymmetry would have to be imposed by hand.

### 2.3 Rejected

**PRISMA and its JobNego dataset (arXiv 2604.18354)** — no utility tables at all. The released sample carries seven columns of dialogue text, emotion, strategy, and rationale; issues appear only as prose inside a free-text scenario blurb and are never scored. The dialogues are fully synthetic and seeded from NegoChat and CaSiNo, the full release is pointed at a resources page that lists no negotiation datasets, and the paper's contribution is emotion-aware reasoning rather than the data. Nothing in it can carry a reward or an opponent-estimate score.

**INA / IND (arXiv 2310.18207)** — a dead end. It is the right framing for e-commerce (bundle-based deals turning distributive haggling into integrative negotiation) but there are no per-party cardinal utility tables, the training reward is hand-designed terms over dialogue acts, no public repository exists, and the distribution page is unreachable. Cite it for the framing; do not plan on the data.

**Conversational-recommendation corpora** — no seller payoff anywhere. ReDial (arXiv 1812.07617, 10,006 dialogues, CC BY 4.0) has only an ordinal liked/disliked label at 81 percent liked. INSPIRED (arXiv 2009.14306, 1,001 dialogues) has the best analogue of CaSiNo's tactic scheme — eight sociable strategies plus two preference-elicitation inquiries — and a post-conversation rating, but the rating is of a movie trailer and there are 12 outright rejections in the whole corpus; its repository shows no license, which needs checking before anything derived ships. DuRecDial 2.0 (arXiv 2109.08877) has the most machine-usable annotation (goal sequences and profiles) and an unverified license. U-NEED (arXiv 2305.04774) is the most realistic — real pre-sales conversations with explicit needs annotation — but its repository returned 404 when checked on 2026-08-11 and no license statement was found, so availability is a live risk. SalesBot (arXiv 2204.10591 and successors) is machine-generated with no utilities. GoRECDIAL (arXiv 1909.03922) is the structurally interesting one — a scored recommendation game trained by supervised imitation then play against a simulated partner — and turning it competitive would mean attaching a payment to each candidate item. VOGUE (arXiv 2510.21151) is the only corpus with two-sided item ratings including the assistant's estimate of the seeker's rating, which is a published design precedent for an opponent estimate, at 60 dialogues far too small to train on. Every one of these is a cooperative recommender: not one has a walk-away with a score or an advisor who benefits from a worse recommendation, so imitating them would teach the wrong objective.

**Used Car Salesbots (arXiv 2605.31445)** — single price. Despite the title nothing about cars is negotiated: it is a simultaneous-offer game over one price for one commodity with buyer and seller reservation values, 4,561 generated scenarios across four information regimes, code released. Keep it as the citation for the finding that fine-tuning agents on financial utility improves deals while degrading honesty — the risk a counterpart-utility term is meant to counteract — and not as an environment.

**Other single-issue 2026 entrants, logged and rejected as environments.** PrefBench (arXiv 2605.22855) prices a frozen vehicle bundle. TERMS-Bench (arXiv 2605.13909) is a scalar-price simulator whose belief-elicitation metric and scripted fixed counterpart are both worth borrowing. MERIT / AgoraBench (arXiv 2602.10467) is buyer-side price bargaining with a composite metric; note that it does **not** score opponent-utility estimates against ground truth, contrary to a summary that circulated during this survey and was checked against the paper. Instructing language models to negotiate with verifiable rewards (arXiv 2604.09855) trains a buyer against a regulated seller on surplus-derived rewards and reports a four-phase strategic evolution ending in linguistic persuasion; its "ten times" headline is a **model-size** ratio against the frontier models it is compared to, not a surplus multiple. SalesLLM (arXiv 2604.07054) scores sales-process progression with a rater and has no utilities at all.

## 3. Augmentation precedent, and the principle underneath it

**Verdict.** Rewriting a negotiation corpus's surface domain while holding its utility mechanics fixed is a published, peer-reviewed, cited move — done first by CaSiNo's own authors — and the one constraint every precedent misses is that the generated numbers must be rewritten in lockstep with the words.

**Opponent Modeling in Negotiation Dialogues by Related Data Adaptation (arXiv 2205.00344).** Chawla and colleagues take Deal or No Deal dialogues and rewrite them into CaSiNo's surface domain by randomly mapping issues across the two datasets and replacing every mention with regular expressions, then simply concatenating the adapted data into training. Their own justification is the sentence to cite: both corpora instantiate the multi-issue bargaining task abstraction, which they describe as a generic framework applicable well beyond these two datasets and name salary negotiation as a legitimate instantiation, in the same breath as JobInterview. Two consequences: surface-domain rewriting is not a reviewer red flag, and a language-model rewrite that also adjusts framing sentences is a strict superset of the regular-expression version they published.

**GNOME (arXiv 2406.10764).** The stronger recipe, and it used JobInterview as one of four seed corpora alongside CaSiNo, CraigslistBargain, and Persuasion for Good. Method: map each corpus's strategy labels onto a common five-label set, correct label skew by inverse-frequency scoring, then feed each whole dialogue to a large model at temperature 1 with instructions to re-skin the setting while preserving style and the exact turn count, with a per-utterance end token so labels transfer automatically. Whole-dialogue mapping beat turn-by-turn mapping on consistency; repeating over a 1,000-dialogue seed produced 472 distinct domains, counted by clustering embeddings of the generated domain titles.

**The limitation that matters, and the principle it implies.** GNOME preserves turn count so that turn labels transfer, and preserves nothing numeric, because its downstream task is strategy classification. A GNOME-style re-skin of any utility-bearing corpus silently breaks the utility tables unless the renaming is applied to them in lockstep. The fix is small but must be designed in: force the rewrite to emit an explicit old-name-to-new-name mapping for every issue and option, apply it to the tables in the same step, then re-score each mapped dialogue's accepted agreement under the mapped table and assert the score is unchanged — a hard automatic check that catches every silent breakage.

Underneath both precedents sits the principle this project should state once and then obey everywhere: **generate the ground truth, then express it in language.** The environment draws the priority tables; the players speak about them; nothing numeric is ever recovered from text by annotation. That is what makes the reward exact, what makes an opponent estimate scorable against truth rather than against a label, and what every rejected corpus in section 2.3 lacks. It is also why a re-skin is legitimate at all: the words are a surface over a generated ledger, so rewriting the surface changes the domain without touching the science — provided the ledger is rewritten with it.

## 4. Implications for this project

**Now: CaSiNo rules as a generative environment.** Three issues, three packages each, 5/4/3 points by private priority order, walk-away pays 5, free-language dialogue. Reimplement the rules rather than replay the corpus: the scenario generator is a permutation and the scorer is a lookup table, so unlimited fresh episodes cost nothing and the corpus stays available for the supervised stage and for human anchors. Train on the natural mix of conflict-alignment levels and stratify the analysis by level, with the identical-orders stratum (ceiling 36) doubling as the near-zero-sum diagnostic — the stratum where section 1.4's degeneracy argument predicts the welfare weight should stop moving surplus share smoothly. The known structural cost is that the game is symmetric, so no asymmetric-role claim can be made from it; the known measurement cost is that the opponent estimate is a six-way ranking rather than a cardinal quantity, which is exactly why framing.md scores it by ranking agreement.

**Later: sales scenarios seeded from CraigslistBargain listings.** Real items give the language something to be about and the paper a second domain, and the precedent for the move is published (arXiv 2205.00344 for the rewrite, arXiv 2406.10764 for the recipe). The hidden weights are generated by us, never scraped: ground truth is always generated. The lockstep constraint from section 3 is not optional — every re-skinned scenario must re-score identically under its re-skinned table before it is used.

**Evaluation only: the [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md) scenarios, zero-shot.** Its seven conflict-of-interest scenarios, each grounded in a Gricean maxim and in advertising regulation, are a ready-made external-validity checklist for a trained persuasion profile, and its logistic decomposition — base propensity plus separate sensitivities to user utility and to company utility — is the steerability instrument framing.md already adopts, fitted before and after training. Two reasons to keep it strictly as zero-shot evaluation and never as training material: it has no environment, no utilities, and no per-episode hidden type, so there is nothing to train against; and its reviewers, on a borderline workshop acceptance, flagged single-domain generalizability, a maximally extreme conflict design, and an unvalidated judge — criticisms that stand unanswered in the record. Transfer to it is a bonus result, not a claim.

**What the dataset choice buys the claim, in one line each.** Two-sided exact utilities make the reward and the axiom check arithmetic rather than judgment. Per-episode hidden priority orders give the opponent estimate something real to be scored against, which is the mechanism sub-claim. Three conflict-alignment levels give efficiency room to vary, which is what makes "efficiency stays high" falsifiable instead of tautological. A non-zero walk-away keeps surplus share well-defined and keeps the deal-versus-no-deal failure mode — the one Deal or No Deal and the bilateral-trade paper both hit — visible in the measurements rather than hidden inside them.
