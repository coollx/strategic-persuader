# Positioning — the competitive landscape

**Question**: who occupies the neighborhood of a steerable, reinforcement-learning-trained, multi-issue language-model negotiator with measured opponent modeling, and how does this project differ from each neighbor?

**Sources**: distilled from the scratch notes `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-10/{seed-papers,methods-novelty}.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/{janus-read,foresight-novelty,advisor-landscape,richer-datasets}.md`, and `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-12/opinion-change-utilities.md`; linked reference analyses are [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md), [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md), [Strategic-Persuasion](../related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md), [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md), and [Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md), whose statements override the scratch notes wherever the two disagree.

## Verdict

The cell this project claims is unoccupied, but it is tightly ringed. Ten systems sit one step away, and no two of them are one step away in the same direction: one owns inference-time steerability without any counterpart-utility term, one owns a two-sided welfare reward without a counterpart to infer, one owns a trained opponent module without a utility to model, one owns the exact environment and the exact ground-truth probe without any reinforcement learning, and one owns the negative result that stated opponent estimates do not convert into better outcomes. The composition — a welfare weight (λ) carried by a prose persuasion profile, optimized by reinforcement learning, in multi-turn free language, over a hidden multi-issue priority table, with the opponent estimate scored against ground truth — is what nobody has. Two of the ten separations are fragile against a plausible revision by their authors, and those two carry clocks; the rest are structural.

Priority already lost, and worth conceding in the first sentence of any related-work paragraph: steerability itself. [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) demonstrated one checkpoint continuously steerable at inference across negotiation postures, and demonstrated that such a scalar is causally active rather than decorative. Steerability is this project's mechanism, not its result.

One of the five linked references is a resource rather than a neighbor: [Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md) supplies the JobInterview corpus and its per-side cardinal scoring function, is a corpus-and-detector paper with no trained negotiator and no welfare term, and therefore appears here only as an environment asset, not as a competitor.

## The neighborhood

### Janus-Protocol

**What it does.** Competitive, Cooperative, and Everything In-Between: The Janus Protocol for Strategy-Infused Language Models (Research Square preprint rs-9000725) — [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) — freezes a Qwen2-7B backbone and attaches low-rank adapters whose per-rank gates are produced by a three-layer network reading one scalar, the normalized settlement position within the zone of possible agreement at episode end. Training is outcome-conditioned behavior cloning: 10,000 script-versus-script trajectories drawn from a pool of 23 hand-coded strategies, every turn of an episode stamped with that episode's terminal scalar, optimized by masked causal cross-entropy plus a gate separation regularizer that stops the network from ignoring the scalar. The domain is bilateral single-price negotiation over at most 20 alternating offers, and the model emits no natural language at all — the action space is an offer price or an accept, expressed over eleven added special tokens, with the offer history encoded as side-and-normalized-price pairs. The evaluation is unreliable as a source of numbers: every evaluation opponent is a training-pool strategy, the headline utility metric is never defined, and the reported aggregate rows are averaged over different opponent sets; the reuse mechanism, not the numbers, is what should be cited.

**What it occupies.** The priority claim on one trained artifact whose negotiation posture is a continuous inference-time control input, plus the role-inversion ablation establishing that the control is causal and the midpoint configuration establishing that it can induce deliberate fairness in division.

**Separations.**
1. Control semantics: their scalar is an outcome coordinate — where on the bargaining line the deal should land — whereas the welfare weight (λ) is a coefficient inside the reward mixing own points and buyer points. In a single-price fixed-width domain the two are the same number by construction and they could not have distinguished them; with three issues and private priority orders they come apart, because λ = 0.5 selects an efficient allocation found by trading issues, not a midpoint split.
2. Training signal: their behavioral ceiling is the 23-strategy pool being imitated, with no environment interaction, no exploration, and no reward; this project runs supervised fine-tuning then group relative policy optimization (GRPO) against a live environment and can exceed its demonstrators.
3. Interface: their conditioning is dual — the scalar as prompt text and as a tensor into per-layer weight-space gates — whereas the persuasion profile is prose in the system prompt and the number is visible only to the reward.
4. Output: numeric offer tokens with no reasoning trace and no argumentation, versus multi-turn free natural language carrying an explicit opponent estimate.
5. Measurement: no opponent modeling by construction, since prices are deliberately normalized against the public range so that private reservation structure cannot be encoded; this project scores the opponent estimate by ranking agreement against the true priority order.
6. Environment: a single price with a fixed-width agreement zone is exactly zero-sum, so their "cooperative" pole can only mean settling at the midpoint — fairness in division, not value creation. Training here spans all conflict-alignment levels, and efficiency is a real quantity with room to move.
7. Credit assignment: stamping every turn with the episode's terminal outcome attributes opponent-caused variance to the policy, so a soft episode against a rigid opponent teaches "to reach a low settlement, play softly"; this project's reward is computed on the realized outcome under the agent's own actions.

**What we borrow.** Concede the steerability priority explicitly and early — a reviewer who knows this preprint will find it anyway, and conceding costs nothing while buying credibility for the contrasts that follow. Mirror their role-inversion ablation as a λ-inversion sanity check and cite them for the design rather than presenting it as new. Take their scalar-collapse failure mode seriously as a risk to check for, and take their base-model diagnosis — an alignment-tuned model capitulates to firm scripted opponents — as motivation. Do not use their numbers as a baseline or a target; if a head-to-head is demanded, the honest answer is that single-issue zero-sum price division and multi-issue bargaining are not commensurable and that they released nothing to run against.

### GTAlign

**What it does.** GTAlign: Game-Theoretic Alignment of LLM Assistants for Social Welfare (arXiv 2510.08872) — [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) — makes an assistant write a payoff matrix over a two-by-three strategy space inside a tagged reasoning chain, derive the efficient frontier from its own matrix, pick a cell by an explicit tie-break rule, and only then answer. The reward is the geometric mean of user welfare and assistant welfare, each a fixed convex combination of measurable factors — answer quality, length regularization, latency, format and matrix-solving correctness — with the coefficients chosen before training so that welfare definitions do not entangle with optimization dynamics. Training is a cold-start supervised stage on teacher-synthesized chains followed by proximal policy optimization (PPO) on roughly 9,000 questions across writing, mathematics, ambiguous questions, and safety. Inference-time steering works by halting generation at the payoff tag, editing the matrix, and resuming, so the provider's cost trade-off becomes an auditable matrix edit.

**What it occupies.** The only published case of a two-sided continuous welfare aggregate used as an actual reinforcement learning reward for a language model, together with a reasoning chain that states both parties' utilities before choosing, and an axiomatic argument for a non-linear aggregator.

**Separations.**
1. Two parties but one turn: a single-turn assistant task, not a multi-turn negotiation against a counterpart with its own hidden priorities, its own walk-away, and the option to leave.
2. The user's welfare is a designer-chosen function of answer quality, length, and latency — measurable by construction, so nothing has to be inferred. Here the counterpart's utility is a per-episode hidden priority table that must be estimated from conversation.
3. Their aggregation weights are fixed before training and they argue for it, on the grounds that a varying weight makes the reward non-stationary. The answer this project must state explicitly is that λ varies across episodes rather than over training, so the training distribution of λ is stationary even though every episode differs — that is an argument to make, not a concession they granted.
4. Their control point sits inside the reasoning trace after it is written; the model never reasons about its own objective. The persuasion profile sits in the prompt before generation, which is precisely what makes the opponent estimate a probe of the policy's own reasoning rather than of a post-hoc edit.
5. Nothing is scored against hidden ground truth: the matrix-quality term enforces internal consistency and a judge checks the frontier derivation, but no stated quantity is compared to a fact the model could not see.

**What we borrow.** The zero-dominance argument — if either side's welfare goes to zero the reward collapses, so neither party can be ignored — is the strongest published case against a plain linear mix, and it must be answered rather than ignored; this project's answer is the maintained efficiency axiom, which forbids collapse at every profile level by a different route. Their payoff-matrix-in-the-chain format is the direct ancestor of the reasoning trace used here. Their negative result matters independently: reinforcement learning directly on abstract classic games, including Rubinstein bargaining, gave oscillating reward without convergence and only non-systematic transfer, which is a documented failure of the naive approach and the backdrop against which a stable GRPO run on a CaSiNo-rules environment is itself worth reporting. Their reviewer file is the best available map of this neighborhood's attack surface and is treated as such below.

### ToMAP

**What it does.** ToMAP: Training Opponent-Aware LLM Persuaders with Theory of Mind (arXiv 2505.22961) trains a 3B persuader with proximal policy optimization in a three-round debate against a frozen persuadee. Two opponent-modeling modules feed the persuader's prompt as text: a counterclaim predictor the persuader runs on itself, and an external attitude classifier — a frozen text encoder plus a five-way perceptron — predicting the persuadee's stance on each counterclaim. The reward is the persuadee's balanced agreement shift immediately following each persuader turn, asymmetrically normalized, plus format, repetition, and length terms; the authors explicitly decompose the multi-turn conversation into independent single-turn optimizations because direct multi-turn optimization is harder. ToMAP reaches 17.48 average agreement shift against 14.60 for plain reinforcement learning and 11.76 for the untrained 3B, beating GPT-4o at 12.54, and two ablations carry the argument: the same opponent information does nothing for untrained models, and replacing the learned classifier with ground-truth attitudes queried from the persuadee each turn gives only a marginal further gain.

**What it occupies.** The closest architectural precedent — the one paper that trains a small model with reinforcement learning to be opponent-aware in a multi-turn setting, with an explicit opponent module and ablations that isolate what training contributes.

**Separations.**
1. There is no counterpart utility at all: the persuadee is an opinion-holder with a stance, not a payoff-maximizing agent, so there is nothing to weight, no welfare trade-off, and no analogue of a welfare weight.
2. The modeled quantity is an attitude toward a claim rather than a hidden priority order over issues, and there is no ground truth to score it against — the reference is the persuadee's own self-report.
3. The opponent model lives in a separately trained classifier bolted onto the prompt, so it cannot function as a probe of what the policy itself believes; here the estimate is generated inside the trace and is therefore under the policy gradient.
4. No steering control of any kind: one checkpoint, one behavior.
5. Training is single-turn by the authors' own admission, with no long-horizon credit assignment; episodes here are multi-turn with the outcome scored at the end.

**What we borrow.** The ablation design that separates "the opponent information helps" from "training teaches the model to use the opponent information" is the single most transferable thing in the paper, and its analogue belongs in this project's plan: give opponent-estimate scaffolding to an untrained model and show it does not move behavior. Their oracle comparison — a cheap estimate nearly matching queried ground truth — is the direct precedent for pricing the oracle-versus-inferred gap. Their frozen-counterpart-as-environment setup, and their transfer check against unseen persuadee models, are both standard practice worth copying.

### Strategic-Persuasion

**What it does.** Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models (arXiv 2509.22989) — [Strategic-Persuasion](../related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md), published at ICLR 2026 — repurposes four human persuasion corpora into games where both sender and receiver are language models, the receiver's action is a seven-point stance and the sender's payoff is that stance's position on the scale. It evaluates seven sender models against a fixed receiver over 475 instances, validates the receiver proxy with a 45-participant human study, then trains a 3B sender with proximal policy optimization and group relative policy optimization against a frozen 8B receiver on roughly 2,700 instances, with reward equal to realized persuasion gain minus a prior-optimal baseline. Trained dynamic gains roughly double, from 0.21 to 0.38, and improve against unseen receiver models. Its analysis finds gains largest at intermediate receiver priors and reads falling cross-turn message similarity in larger models as adaptive disclosure.

**What it occupies.** The sender-and-receiver vocabulary, reinforcement learning against a frozen receiver as environment dynamics, and a persuasion metric derived from theory rather than from a judge.

**Separations.** The load-bearing fact first: the receiver utility is notation and a prompt sentence, never computed in any experiment and never entering the reward, and the receiver's objective is a fixed truth-tracking loss identical across every instance.
1. Nothing about the receiver varies per instance — what varies is the claim, the prior, and the drawn state — so there is nothing for the sender to infer about *this* counterpart. Their information asymmetry is over the world state; this project's is over preferences.
2. No counterpart-welfare term appears in the reward and there is no steering control; the reward is pure sender gain against a prior baseline.
3. Structurally, the receiver's stance *is* the sender's payoff, so there is only one number on the table. Leaving the receiver a margin would mean the sender simply scoring lower with nothing on the other side of the ledger; a welfare weight is meaningful only when the counterpart could rationally want something other than to be maximally accurate.
4. Episodes are one or three stylized debate rounds in which the receiver replies with a stance score and a question — not a negotiation with a deal, a walk-away, and an efficiency frontier.
5. No opponent estimate is elicited, and none is scored.

**What we borrow.** The frozen-receiver-as-environment design and, more importantly, its transfer check across receiver models never seen in training, which this project reproduces with held-out buyer models. Their human-study protocol for validating a language model standing in for a human belief-updater is reusable whenever a prompted counterpart carries a claim. Their finding that interaction structure matters more than raw model capability supports spending budget on multi-turn episodes rather than on a larger backbone. And this paper is what lets the whole opinion-change family be closed in one sentence: no opinion-change benchmark computes a receiver utility or puts one in a reward, and the single paper that writes one down defines it as a fixed truth-tracking loss identical across instances.

### BOND

**What it does.** Distilling Bayesian Belief States into Language Models for Auditable Negotiation (arXiv 2605.04507) works in this project's exact environment. A language module scores the dialogue's compatibility with each of the six possible opponent priority orderings; a Bayesian module updates a posterior over those orderings each turn; a menu module enumerates all feasible allocations and scores each by own utility plus a coefficient times expected opponent utility under the posterior; a decision module chooses among accept, counter, submit, walk away, and utter. The teacher's trajectories are distilled by a low-rank adapter into an 8B student that emits the posterior and its selected intent as tagged text. The posterior is scored mechanically against the true hidden ordering by Brier score — teacher 0.085, distilled student 0.114, uniform six-ordering reference 0.139, and a prompted structured-reasoning baseline 0.194, which is worse than uniform — and the paper's headline negative finding is that causal belief-action coupling remains limited after supervised distillation: the student reports a good belief and then does not act on it.

**What it occupies.** The nearest occupant overall — the same environment, the enumerate-and-score-both-sides selection rule, and a mechanical ground-truth probe of a stated belief.

**Separations.**
1. Training is supervised distillation only; there is no reinforcement learning anywhere in the paper, and the weak belief-action coupling they report is exactly the deficit a policy-gradient stage is supposed to fix. This is an explicit, published opening for the intervention proposed here.
2. The both-party numbers are computed in an external symbolic module with exact arithmetic, and the student externalizes a six-way posterior rather than per-candidate utility estimates. Generating those numbers inside the trace is what places them under the policy gradient and makes the probe a probe of the policy rather than of a bolted-on module.
3. Their coefficient is fixed at 1.0 and explicitly disowned as a steering control — the paper says the additive form makes it an opponent-utility exchange rate rather than a bounded self-other interpolation, and declines to treat conditioning on it as a performance claim. The welfare weight (λ) here is sampled per episode, carried by a prose persuasion profile, and enters the reward.
4. Evaluation is turn-level replay against held-out human transcripts; this project rolls out live against a buyer with a real walk-away, so efficiency and surplus share are outcomes rather than replayed labels.
5. The probed quantity is a six-way posterior; the opponent estimate here is a stated ranking scored by ranking agreement, which is finer-grained and strictly harder to get right.

**What we borrow.** The Brier-score calibration instrument, and — more valuable — the belief-versus-policy error decomposition that separates "wrong belief" from "right belief, wrong action". That decomposition is precisely what the mechanism sub-claim needs, and it is what turns the strongest external negative result in this neighborhood into a measurable question rather than an objection. Their prompted-baseline number, an elicited posterior worse than uniform, is the best published evidence that untrained opponent estimates have headroom, which speaks directly to the fourth falsifier.

### ASTRA

**What it does.** ASTRA (arXiv 2503.07129) is a prompted three-stage negotiation agent on CaSiNo: interpret the counterpart's behavior, optimize counteroffers through a tool-integrated linear program, then select offers by strategy assessment and estimated partner acceptance probability. Its inferred-partner-preferences module estimates the partner's score numerically as the inner product of item quantities and inferred preferences, and its linear program carries a coefficient in the unit interval balancing own utility against partner utility in the objective. The paper's selling point is that it operates without any training data. Reported results are 24.31 points with the full system against 20.75 without it and 18.86 for a reinforcement-learning baseline, with human evaluation 2.91 against 1.98 out of 4.

**What it occupies.** The same environment and the same trade-off-coefficient semantics — the closest neighbor on surface features, and the reason a reviewer may ask what training buys.

**Separations.**
1. Nothing is trained; this project is supervised fine-tuning then GRPO.
2. Their coefficient is endogenous and adaptive, set by reciprocity from the detected partner stance — roughly 0.9 against a greedy partner and 0.3 against a generous one. It is the agent's tactic, not an operator's control. The welfare weight (λ) here is supplied per episode by the persuasion profile, sampled independently of everything else in the scenario.
3. The numeric optimization is delegated to a linear-programming solver, so the visible reasoning carries no line-by-line both-party utility figures and there is nothing in the text to score.
4. Partner-preference accuracy is never measured against the true hidden priority order; the only check is an internal-consistency checker looking for contradictions.

**What we borrow.** It sets the bar. A prompted agent with an external solver already performs well in this environment, which makes an ASTRA-style prompted arm a required baseline rather than an optional one, and its reported points give a rough scale for what is achievable under CaSiNo rules. Its adaptive coefficient is also a useful contrast to name in one sentence: an agent that sets its own trade-off is doing tactics, whereas a profile-set weight is doing control.

### Counterparty Modeling is Not Strategy

**What it does.** Counterparty Modeling is Not Strategy (arXiv 2605.16575, Salesforce AI Research) builds a controlled bilateral car-purchase domain with ten negotiation terms — four continuous, five categorical, one binary — with linear private utilities whose weights are drawn per feature group under role-specific sign constraints, a reservation value equal to the utility of the worst feasible contract, and reported utility normalized between walk-away and ideal. Experiments run on Qwen3-235B-A22B and replicate on DeepSeek-R1-671B, with reasoning traces logged. The finding is that agents model counterparty preferences accurately and early in their traces but do not convert that into better agreements: outcomes track opening anchors rather than utility weights, and an explicit concession-for-reciprocity template makes turns look strategic without improving final efficiency.

**What it occupies.** The sharpest published negative result on exactly the quantity this project proposes to measure. It is a competitor to the headline claim rather than a resource, and it must be cited and answered rather than mentioned in passing.

**Separations.**
1. Purely prompted frontier models with no training of any kind. The claim here is that supervised fine-tuning followed by GRPO closes the modeling-to-strategy gap that prompting does not — a claim their design can neither make nor refute.
2. No steering control: one behavior, no welfare weight, no profile, so there is no axis along which the use of the estimate could vary.
3. Their measurement is descriptive — traces contain accurate preference statements — and their outcome measure is agreement quality; they report no per-episode estimate-accuracy score that can be regressed against a training stage or a profile level.
4. Their domain is a ten-term contract with continuous terms; CaSiNo rules give three issues and six possible priority orders, which makes the opponent estimate exactly scoreable and the conflict-alignment level a clean stratifying variable.

**What we borrow.** Their result is the reason the opponent-modeling claim is framed here as a mechanism sub-claim rather than a headline: measuring stated opponent estimates is not enough on its own, and estimate accuracy and estimate use must be reported as two separate numbers, with BOND's decomposition supplying the instrument. Their domain is also a clean reimplementation of roughly a hundred lines if a second environment is ever wanted, and reimplementing it would give a direct comparison against the strongest published negative result in this lane.

### Truthful AI Advisors

**What it does.** Truthful AI Advisors: A Pre-Specified Benchmark for Large Language Model Honesty Under Preference Misalignment (arXiv 2606.01456) instantiates classical cheap talk as an alignment benchmark. A sender observes a state in the unit interval, is told a bias and the payoff rule, and emits one short message; a calibrated decoder maps the message to an action, and an oracle computes the most-informative partition for the same bias. The pre-specified design is five bias levels, three prompt frames, 200 seeded states per cell, four models, and 12,000 sender calls. All four models massively over-reveal — normalized mutual information of 0.78 to 0.94 where the oracle prescribes 0.18 to 0.53 — and the mechanism is linear exaggeration: the sender reports approximately the state plus its bias, with estimated partition counts stuck at 2.00 regardless of bias, and payoff-maximizing versus honesty framing has negligible effect. Code, all 12,000 logged messages, and the analysis scripts are released.

**What it occupies.** The single closest precedent for a payoff parameter stated in the sender's prompt, and the best-instrumented evaluation in the neighborhood.

**Separations.**
1. Strictly one-shot — a single message, named by the authors themselves as a limitation.
2. Nothing is hidden: the sender is told the bias, the payoff rule, and the receiver's ideal action, so there is no opponent modeling and no quantity to estimate.
3. No training whatsoever; it is a black-box evaluation of instruction-tuned frontier models.
4. The bias parameterizes a fixed quadratic payoff rather than a mixture over own and counterpart utility, and no policy is ever optimized against it.
5. The receiver is a statistical decoder rather than an agent with its own utility and a walk-away.

**What we borrow.** This is the load-bearing citation for the framing claim that instructions alone barely move sender behavior: it is direct, well-powered evidence that a payoff parameter in the system prompt does not steer an untrained model, and it is therefore the argument for training the welfare weight rather than prompting it. Its decoder ablation carries a second lesson worth heeding — the finding survives only if the instrument reads the right channel, since an embedding-only decoder misread the same data as near-babbling and failed the zero-bias sanity check. Its comprehension diagnostic, separating models that over-reveal despite understanding the game from models that may merely be echoing, is a template for the capability control described below.

### Chatbot-Ads

**What it does.** Ads in AI Chatbots? An Analysis of How Large Language Models Navigate Conflicts of Interest (arXiv 2604.08525) — [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md) — crosses Grice's conversational maxims with advertising regulation to define seven conflict-of-interest scenarios, then runs 23 deployed models in a flight-booking setting where the system prompt softly asks the model to prioritize sponsored airlines priced roughly twice the alternatives. Eighteen of 23 models recommend the sponsored flight over half the time; sponsored recommendations skew toward users who appear wealthy, 64% against 49%; no false statements about flight features are detected anywhere, but sponsorship status is concealed at a mean rate of 0.65; and explicit steering instructions move most models in the instructed direction while a few move against it. It was accepted as a poster at the ICLR 2026 workshop on algorithmic fairness in alignment procedures and agentic systems, on an explicitly borderline, discussion-motivated meta-review.

**What it occupies.** The evaluation side of the welfare trade-off — what untrained models do when the operator's interest and the user's interest diverge — and the regression instrument this project's framing already cites.

**Separations.**
1. Evaluation only; nobody trains the dial. This is precisely the paper a training-side result answers.
2. Single-turn prompted vignettes with no environment: no per-episode hidden utility, no walk-away, no efficiency measure, and an incentive that exists only as prompt text — a limitation the authors acknowledge and reviewers pressed.
3. Their instrument is a two-parameter logistic decomposition — a base propensity plus separate sensitivities to user utility and company utility — chosen because the constrained one-parameter alternative, a single weight on user utility and its complement on company utility, fits worse by McFadden R² and yields a weight outside the unit interval for some models. That is the strongest published evidence for the first half of this project's claim: untrained models do not admit a single coherent welfare weight. The claim here is that a trained one does.
4. Their tension axis is price alone, with sponsorship and price deliberately confounded, so "recommends sponsored" always also means "recommends more expensive"; here the frontier is multi-issue and efficiency and surplus share are separable quantities.

**What we borrow.** Fit their regression — base propensity plus separate sensitivities to each side's utility — before and after training, as the steerability instrument the framing already specifies. Take their empirical ordering across the Gricean maxims (no outright lies, heavy framing and concealment) as a prediction of where optimization pressure will surface first in a trained persuader. Reuse the soft-instruction design as the untrained baseline condition that isolates default disposition rather than instruction compliance. Their reviewer file supplies two defenses adopted below.

### LLMs Persuade Without Planning Theory of Mind

**What it does.** Large Language Models Persuade Without Planning Theory of Mind (arXiv 2602.17045) builds an interactive task in which a persuader must move a target to one of three proposals by selective disclosure, with payoff matrices generated by a constraint solver so that both full disclosure and no disclosure fail and only a targeted two-fact intervention succeeds. The central manipulation is whether the persuader sees the target's value function and current knowledge or must infer them. Against a naively rational bot target that is immune to rhetoric by construction, o3 succeeds 5% of the time in the hidden condition — below the conservative chance baseline — against 65% when the target's values are revealed, while human persuaders score 29% and 22%. Against human targets, o3 reaches 51% hidden; a counterfactual replay feeding each transcript's disclosures to the rational bot shows that both o3 and human persuaders would have succeeded about 2% of the time, so success against humans is largely decoupled from planning about the target's mental state.

**What it occupies.** The dissociation warning: proof that persuasion success and opponent modeling come apart empirically, and the reason task reward cannot serve as evidence of opponent modeling.

**Separations.**
1. No training at all — one frontier model evaluated against human baselines, so there is no policy to steer and no reward to shape.
2. A single-target three-alternative choice over a discrete nine-cell payoff matrix, not open-ended multi-turn bargaining with an efficiency frontier and a walk-away.
3. The target's mental state is a value function revealed or hidden as a binary experimental condition, rather than a per-episode hidden priority order that must be estimated continuously and scored every episode.
4. Nothing the persuader states about the target is scored; the instrument is a counterfactual replay of disclosures, not a direct probe of a stated estimate.

**What we borrow.** This paper is why the mechanism sub-claim takes the form it does — opponent modeling is measured directly and never inferred from task success. Two instruments transfer: a scripted rational buyer as a control arm alongside the prompted language-model buyer, which is already in the framing's evaluation plan, and the replay idea, which separates "did the seller's behavior constitute a correct intervention on the buyer's actual priorities" from "did the buyer happen to comply". Their revealed-versus-hidden contrast is also the design of the oracle-versus-inferred gap.

## Reviewer-tested attack surfaces and the defenses adopted

Two neighbors have public reviewer records, and between them they cover most of what this project will be attacked for. GTAlign's rejection maps the attack surface for a two-sided welfare reward with a structured reasoning chain; Chatbot-Ads' borderline acceptance maps the attack surface for a behavioral claim measured by judges under confounded conditions. Both records are authoritative here: they were retrieved through the authenticated OpenReview interface and recorded in the linked reference analyses.

**GTAlign — rejected at ICLR 2026 with ratings 2, 2, 2, 8; the area chair discounted one negative review as likely machine-written, and the sole positive review was the lowest-confidence one.** Four attacks, each with a defense adopted here.

*Framing.* One reviewer argued the method is an optimization procedure rather than a game — the user is not a strategic player, no equilibrium concept is actually applied, and the "sequential game" is really a one-shot matrix — and the meta-review agreed the work reads as a reinforcement learning formulation rather than a genuine game-theoretic one. Invoking a textbook definition in rebuttal moved nothing. Defense: this project's framing already refuses equilibrium claims outright, stating that the seller computes a best response to a fixed buyer population. That sentence belongs in the abstract, not in an appendix, and game-theoretic vocabulary the design does not earn should not be used anywhere.

*Missing same-reward-no-trace baseline.* A reviewer demanded a reinforcement learning baseline trained on the identical reward but without the tagged reasoning format; the authors declined on the grounds that the structured chain, not the welfare score, was the contribution, and the demand resurfaced verbatim in the meta-review. Defense: run it. A GRPO arm trained on the identical welfare-weighted reward with no opponent estimate in the trace is a required arm rather than an optional ablation, and it is also the cleanest attribution test available — it separates "the welfare weight steers behavior" from "the reasoning format helps".

*Scale.* The same reviewer asked for models of at least 7B and the authors answered with compute limits. Defense: the framing already specifies a 3-8B open model; land at the upper end of that band, or report at least two sizes so the scale question is answered with data. Never answer it with a sentence about economy.

*Statistics.* A reviewer noted that no table reported variance or confidence intervals. Defense: every headline number carries an interval over episodes and seeds; the profile-level curve carries a per-level interval; and the steerability claim is reported as a fitted slope with an interval rather than as a row of point estimates. This matters doubly because the other close neighbor fails on statistics in the mirror-image way — Janus reports an analysis of variance over unpaired draws from different opponent sets with no intervals on any mean — so the reviewer pool in this area is primed on exactly this weakness.

**Chatbot-Ads — accepted as a workshop poster with ratings 4, 3, 2 and a meta-review recording consensus on timeliness but "mixed final verdicts due to the execution", with acceptance recommended for the discussion the paper could spark; no author responses appear in the forum, so every criticism stands unanswered.** Two attacks, plus a lesson about rebuttals.

*Judge validation.* Two reviewers objected that a frontier model judges positive framing and concealment with no human-annotation validation, and one added that self-preference bias — a judge scoring outputs of closely related models more favorably — is neither acknowledged nor tested. Defense: keep judges out of the measurement path entirely. Efficiency and surplus share are computed from the ground-truth point tables, and the opponent estimate is scored by ranking agreement against the true priority order, so the two headline columns and the mechanism probe are all arithmetic. Wherever a judge cannot be avoided, validate it against human labels on a sample and report the agreement rate alongside the number it produced.

*The confound triad.* The most critical review raised three: a model declining the sponsored option may simply be failing to follow the instruction rather than protecting the user, and no non-moral instruction-following control was run; randomly tagging real airlines as sponsored creates trials where world knowledge conflicts with the stimulus, so refusals may reflect confusion; and low-status personas disproportionately carry urgent scenarios, so the measured effect may be a scenario feature rather than a stance. Generalized, the triad says any behavioral difference attributed to a stance can instead be capability, world knowledge, or a correlated scenario feature. Three defenses are adopted. First, a capability control: verify the model can execute the requested behavior at all, so that a flat curve at a proself profile is read as steering failure only after instruction-following failure has been excluded. Second, world-knowledge neutrality: hidden priorities are always generated, never recovered from language, and CaSiNo's items are neutral goods, so no brand or world prior can carry the effect. Third, the persuasion profile is sampled independently of everything else in the scenario — this is already in the framing, and it is exactly what breaks a confound of the urgency kind, because nothing in the scenario correlates with the profile by construction.

*Two further reviewer themes worth pre-empting.* Reviewers wanted a range of conflict intensities rather than the single maximally extreme setting, and wanted more than one domain; stratifying analysis by conflict-alignment level is this project's version of the first, and the sales instantiation seeded from real listings is its version of the second. Reviewers also noted that a prompt-level incentive says nothing about incentives implemented through training — which is the gap this project fills, and is worth stating in one sentence rather than leaving implicit. Finally, the absence of any author response in that forum is its own lesson: unanswered criticisms become the record.

## The unoccupied cell

**The claim, stated as a conjunction.** One checkpoint, trained by supervised fine-tuning followed by group relative policy optimization, whose per-episode scalar reward mixes the seller's own points and the buyer's points at a welfare weight (λ) set by a prose persuasion profile in the system prompt; acting in multi-turn free natural language over a multi-issue negotiation with a per-episode hidden priority table on the counterpart's side and a real walk-away; whose reasoning trace states an opponent estimate that is scored by ranking agreement against the ground-truth priority order; reported as two columns, efficiency held high at every profile level and surplus share as the quantity the profile steers, stratified by conflict-alignment level. Six ingredients, each individually precedented; the composition is unoccupied. The narrowest and cleanest way to say what is unclaimed is that the utility estimates in the reasoning are simultaneously the mechanism and the measurement instrument — the opponent-modeling probe is free because the trace already contains the numbers.

**Why each neighbor cannot reach it in a plausible revision.**

Janus-Protocol cannot measure an opponent estimate without the model emitting utility estimates, which requires a reasoning trace, which means abandoning the slot-filled action-token format that is the entire architecture — a rewrite, not a revision. Their control scalar also does not survive a move to multiple issues: a normalized settlement position has no meaning once there is no single line to divide, so a multi-issue version must invent a new control variable, and the obvious one is a welfare weight. Every revision their own future-work section names — boundary smoothing, larger adapter rank, curriculum weighting of extreme values, held-out opponents, defining the utility metric — is neutral for this project.

GTAlign's user is a dataset, not a counterpart with hidden priorities, and their reward is well-defined precisely because both sides' welfare is computable without inference. Introducing a hidden per-instance preference removes the property their whole reward design rests on, and giving the counterpart a walk-away and multiple turns means building a negotiation environment they do not have.

ToMAP's persuadee has a stance, not a payoff, so there is no second number to weight and a welfare weight has no referent. Supplying one means changing the task rather than the method.

Strategic-Persuasion faces the same blockage stated more sharply: the receiver's stance *is* the sender's payoff, and the receiver objective is a fixed truth-tracking loss identical across instances, so both the second number and the per-instance hidden preference are missing. Adding either means leaving opinion change, which their own limitations section already concedes is the scope.

BOND is the most plausible revision in the set, and it is treated as a live risk below. A policy-gradient stage on the distilled student closes the training-signal gap and their own stated limitation names the deficit. Even so, three separations survive: the both-party numbers stay in an external symbolic module unless the architecture changes, their coefficient stays an additive exchange rate they have explicitly declined to treat as a steering control, and evaluation stays turn-level replay unless they stand up a live buyer.

ASTRA would have to be trained, which is a different paper, and its coefficient is endogenous by design — making it operator-supplied removes the reciprocity mechanism that is its contribution. Moving the arithmetic out of the solver and into the text is the same architectural change BOND would need.

Counterparty Modeling is Not Strategy is a negative result about prompted frontier models; a revision that trains would be answering this project's question rather than its own.

Truthful AI Advisors is pre-specified around an exact equilibrium benchmark, which requires the scalar-state, known-bias, quadratic-payoff setting. Hiding the receiver's preference destroys the oracle that makes the benchmark exact, so the thing that would bring it close is the thing that would break it.

Chatbot-Ads explicitly defers incentive channels beyond prompts to future work, and adopting one means training a model against an environment their prompted vignettes do not provide.

LLMs Persuade Without Planning Theory of Mind is a human-subject dissociation result whose value is the human comparison; training a model removes the comparison rather than extending it.

## Scooping risks and their clocks

**BOND version 2 — highest damage, no visible clock, so check most often.** Their published limitation is weak belief-action coupling after supervised distillation, and the obvious fix is a policy-gradient stage on the distilled student. The environment, the probe, and the enumerate-and-score rule are already built and the code is public, so the marginal work for them is small. Re-check arXiv 2605.04507 for a new version, and the repository for a reinforcement learning branch, on a monthly cadence. If it lands, the surviving separations are the in-trace numbers, the steerable welfare weight carried by a persuasion profile, and live rollouts against a buyer with a walk-away — and the paper should be reorganized around those before the fact rather than after.

**Janus-Protocol revision — visible clock, low damage.** Under review at a Springer journal since 2026-03-01: editor assigned 2026-03-03, reviewers invited 2026-06-09, two reviewers agreed on 2026-06-18 and 2026-07-07, and a further review was invited on 2026-07-23. A first decision is plausible within months, so a revised version may appear before submission. Action: re-check the preprint page and the journal before submission, and re-check the author list against any published version, since the third author is Feyza M. Hafızoğlu and earlier notes in this repository had the name wrong.

**The ulab-uiuc group's pace — structural risk, roughly one paper per four months in this area.** Strategic-Persuasion (ICLR 2026), ToMAP, and GTAlign all come from the same University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign lab, and each is one step from this project's design in a different direction: they already train small persuaders with reinforcement learning against frozen counterparts, they already put a two-sided welfare reward inside a reasoning chain, and GTAlign's rejection on framing and missing baselines gives them a concrete reason to move that line of work toward a setting where the second party is a real counterpart with something to lose. The specific move to expect from them is a two-sided welfare reward in a multi-turn negotiation. Action: watch that group's listings, and do not assume the three papers stay in separate lanes.

**Ambient crowding — lower damage, faster movement.** The bargaining lane added several entries within twelve months: a benchmark with a fine-tuning pipeline (arXiv 2602.10467), reinforcement-learning-trained bargaining agents (arXiv 2604.09855 — note that the "ten times" figure sometimes attributed to this paper refers to model size, not surplus, and must not be repeated as a surplus claim), a checkpoint-pool population method on a sibling environment (arXiv 2608.06735), preference estimation by opponent modeling (arXiv 2604.15687), and an evaluation harness scoring opponent modeling among six diagnostics (arXiv 2605.13909). ## Implications for this project

**Lead with the probe and the welfare trade-off; never lead with steerability.** Janus owns steerability as a demonstrated result, so present it as this project's mechanism and credit them for establishing that the mechanism works. The sentence that carries the contribution is that the opponent estimate is simultaneously the mechanism and the measurement instrument.

**Four baseline arms are required, not optional.** A prompted agent in the ASTRA style, because a prompted agent with a solver already performs well in this environment. An untrained prompted model at each profile level, because falsifier one demands it and because Truthful AI Advisors predicts it will barely move. A GRPO arm on the identical reward with no opponent estimate in the trace, because that is the missing-baseline attack that sank GTAlign and because it is the cleanest attribution test. And held-out buyer models plus the scripted rational buyer, because the frozen-counterpart weakness is the one reviewers will find first and because the rational-buyer arm is what separates opponent modeling from counterpart compliance.

**Three instruments come off the shelf.** The two-parameter regression from Chatbot-Ads — base propensity plus separate sensitivities to each side's utility — fitted before and after training, which the framing already names as the steerability instrument and which carries the published finding that untrained models do not admit a single coherent weight. Ranking agreement against the true priority order for the opponent estimate. And BOND's belief-versus-policy decomposition, which is what answers the strongest external objection in this neighborhood: Counterparty Modeling is Not Strategy shows accurate stated estimates that do not convert into better outcomes, so estimate accuracy and estimate use must be reported as two numbers, not one.

**Keep judges out of the measurement path, and state the scale and statistics discipline up front.** Efficiency, surplus share, and the opponent-estimate score are all arithmetic over ground-truth tables; any judge that survives elsewhere gets a human-validation sample and a reported agreement rate. Every headline number carries an interval, the profile curve carries per-level intervals, and the steerability claim is a fitted slope with an interval. Land at the upper end of the 3-8B band or report two sizes.

**Concede early and precisely in related work.** Steerability priority to Janus in the first sentence, before any contrast. The equilibrium disclaimer — best response to a fixed buyer population, no equilibrium claims — in the abstract rather than an appendix. The response to GTAlign's fixed-weight argument stated as a positive claim, that λ varies across episodes and not over training, so the training distribution is stationary. And one neutral sentence on why a single-price zero-sum domain cannot separate a settlement position from a welfare weight, which is what makes this project's control variable a different object rather than a renamed one.

**Two clocks to keep running.** Monthly re-checks of arXiv 2605.04507 and its repository for a reinforcement learning stage, and a pre-submission re-check of the Janus preprint and its journal status, including the author list against any published version.
