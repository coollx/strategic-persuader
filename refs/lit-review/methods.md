# Training and measurement practice for a steerable negotiator

Question: what does published practice establish about how to train a language-model negotiator whose stance is set at inference time, and how to measure whether the stance is actually being followed?

Sources: distilled from `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-10/steering-methods.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-10/honesty-measurement.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-10/profile-personalization.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/control-taxonomy.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/pluralistic-alignment.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/attributes-alignment.md`, `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-11/foresight-novelty.md`, and `scratch/lit-review-2026-08-12/opponent-practice.md`, with the reference analyses [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md), [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md), [Strategic-Persuasion](../related-work/Strategic-Persuasion/paper_analysis.md), [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) and [Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md) authoritative wherever they and the notes disagree.

## 1. Where the control setting can live

Verdict: five mechanism families can set a model's behavioral stance at inference time, and exactly one of them puts the setting somewhere the policy can read during the forward pass that produces its own reasoning. That is prompt-text conditioning. The constraint is not a preference for prompting; it is what makes an in-reasoning opponent estimate a probe of the policy rather than of a bolted-on module, because the same forward pass that writes the estimate has to be able to see the persuasion profile it is estimating under.

| Mechanism family | Representative methods | What is controlled | Granularity | Policy can read the setting | Multi-turn agent evidence | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prompt-text conditioning | SteerLM (arXiv 2310.05344); Rewards-in-Context (arXiv 2402.10207); Directional Preference Alignment (arXiv 2402.18571); MO-ODPO (arXiv 2503.00295); L1/LCPO (arXiv 2503.04697); RC-GRPO (arXiv 2602.03025) | Anything statable in words: named objective levels, target reward values, weight vectors, length targets | Continuous in principle, quantized to one decimal in practice | Yes, natively — the setting is tokens, so it can be quoted, restated and reasoned over | RC-GRPO on multi-turn tool calling; nothing on multi-turn dialogue with a weight vector | High for single-turn, unproven for multi-turn dialogue |
| Parameter-space conditioning | Rewarded Soups (arXiv 2306.04488); Panacea (arXiv 2402.02030); Conditional Language Policy (arXiv 2407.15762); [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) | A weight vector or a scalar posture, injected into weights by interpolation, by singular values of a low-rank adaptation (LoRA) layer, or by a hypernetwork that gates LoRA ranks | Continuous | No — weights are fixed before the forward pass, and no token representation exists | Janus-Protocol is the only negotiation instance, and it emits numeric offer tokens rather than dialogue | Medium |

Three findings independently rule out the activation route for this design even setting reasoning-visibility aside. First, chain of thought fights steering in exactly our regime: in a distilled reasoning model, activation steering reverses refusal in 39 percent of cases when the chain of thought is held fixed, 70 percent when it is removed, and 94 percent when the model regenerates its chain of thought under steering, and the regenerated chain alone retains 48 percent of the effect after the vector is withdrawn (arXiv 2605.26772). A seller that writes a structured estimate before every move is the case where the model's own written reasoning partly cancels the residual-stream intervention. Second, steering gain is highly variable across inputs, driven in part by spurious per-input features, and brittle to reasonable prompt changes out of distribution (arXiv 2407.12404) — which is disqualifying when the headline result is that behavior moves monotonically across a grid of persuasion profiles. Third, the usable coefficient range is small: contrastive activation addition sweeps at plus or minus one and caps open-ended generation at plus or minus two, because larger multipliers degrade text quality.

The reasoning-visibility bound is now precise enough to state as a number rather than a worry. Without training, models notice injected concepts only unreliably and context-dependently (arXiv 2601.01828). With dedicated fine-tuning, seven instruction-tuned models reach up to 95.5 percent detection that a vector was injected and 71.2 percent identification of which concept, with zero false positives — but only for directions geometrically close to the training distribution, and detection does not confer resistance (arXiv 2511.21399). The only measurement of perceived *magnitude* finds models localize which of ten sentences received an injection at up to 88 percent against a 10 percent chance rate, and discriminate relative injection strengths at 83 percent against a 50 percent chance rate, both collapsing to chance outside early layers (arXiv 2512.12411). Reading an absolute value off a grid and applying it as a decision rule on every turn is a different task by more than a matter of degree. Decoding-time and weight-space control are worse than hard: the setting never enters the forward pass at all, so there is no experiment that could make them readable, and a reward-mixture decoder additionally reweights the reasoning tokens themselves, which would contaminate an opponent-estimate probe at its source.

Two naming facts matter for how this gets written up. The umbrella vocabulary comes from A Roadmap to Pluralistic Alignment (arXiv 2402.05070), which distinguishes Overton pluralism (present the spectrum of reasonable answers), **steerable pluralism** (Definition 8: conditioned on an attribute, the model produces a response faithfully reflecting it), and distributional pluralism (match a reference population), and which defines a **trade-off steerable benchmark** (Definition 17) as one that measures both objective attainment and steerability to arbitrary trade-offs — a category the roadmap states does not yet exist for language models. A sweep over persuasion profiles is exactly a trade-off steerable evaluation. The second fact is a collision to avoid: "online alignment" is already taken and means on-policy sampling during reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) training, offline meaning a fixed preference set (arXiv 2405.08448). Using it for a deployment-time control reads as a category error. The precise self-description is that this is a *training* method that yields *inference-time steerability*; "inference-time alignment" in current usage means steering a frozen model with no weight updates, which is not what happens here.

## 2. Multi-objective conditioned training

Verdict: conditioning a policy on a prompted weight and training over sampled weights is a thoroughly established recipe for single-turn generation, with at least eight method papers and two head-to-head Pareto-front comparisons from one industrial group. What is not established is the algorithm we need — online policy-gradient training with the weight in the prompt — and applying it to a multi-turn dialogue agent appears unpublished. The failure modes are documented well enough to design against in advance, and the single most useful result is that reinforcement learning is what installs a prompted knob while prompting and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) do not.

| Method | Conditioning mechanism | Training algorithm | Objectives shown | Turn structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SteerLM (arXiv 2310.05344) | Prompt text: a label string of integers 0-9 per named objective appended to the prompt | Conditioned SFT after labelling the corpus with a prediction model, then bootstrapping on high-scoring samples; no reinforcement learning | 7 named in the label string, steerability shown on 2 | Single-turn |
| Rewards-in-Context (arXiv 2402.10207) | Prompt text: *target reward values* as markers, one decimal each, not weights | Multi-reward conditioned SFT offline, then an online stage augmenting data near the empirical Pareto front by multi-objective rejection sampling | 3 on a helpful/harmless/humour corpus | Single-turn |
| Directional Preference Alignment (arXiv 2402.18571) | Prompt text: preference as a unit vector embedded numerically in the system prompt | Multi-objective reward model plus preference-conditioned rejection-sampling fine-tuning | 2 (helpfulness, verbosity) | Single-turn |
| MO-ODPO (arXiv 2503.00295) | Prompt text: a literal system-instruction prefix naming each objective and its weight, quantized to one decimal | Online direct preference optimization: sample two completions from the current policy, score both with several reward models, scalarize with the sampled weights, use the ordering as the preference pair | 2, on two corpora | Single-turn |
| Rewarded Soups (arXiv 2306.04488) | Parameters: linear interpolation of independently fine-tuned weights, one model per reward | RLHF per reward independently; the interpolation is zero-shot at inference with no multi-objective training | 2 mostly, across text, vision-language and control | Single-turn generation |
| Panacea (arXiv 2402.02030) | Parameters: the preference vector injected as the singular values of low-rank adaptation layers, with a learnable scale | Compatible with SFT, RLHF or direct preference optimization; supports linear scalarization or weighted Tchebycheff aggregation | Up to 10, no saturation reported | Single-turn |
| Conditional Language Policy (arXiv 2407.15762) | Parameters primarily, with a prompt-only variant as their baseline and a combined variant in the appendix | Reinforcement fine-tuning with REINFORCE plus a control variate, multi-task over weights sampled from a Dirichlet distribution over the simplex | 2 and 3 rewards on summarization | Single-turn |
| L1/LCPO (arXiv 2503.04697) | Prompt text: a plain-language target ("think for n tokens") appended to the prompt | Group relative policy optimization (GRPO), reward equal to a correctness indicator minus a scaled absolute deviation from the target length, target drawn uniformly per example | 2 — exactly the one-knob case | Single-turn |

Two conclusions fall out of the table. Prompt-text conditioning is the deployment-friendly choice and everyone agrees why: parameter conditioning needs per-query merging or extra adapters at serving time, which MO-ODPO calls hard to deploy. And every method that reports a front makes the same three moves — sample a weight per example, inject it, scalarize the reward vector with it — disagreeing only about how to sample and where to inject.

**The scalar-per-update resolution.** The apparent paradox — if several objectives matter they must enter the reward, but the reward is one number anyway, so why keep them separate — dissolves once the reward is distinguished from the function that produced it. Every paper surveyed computes a vector of component scores and collapses it to one number before the policy-gradient or preference step: Rewarded Soups, Rewards-in-Context, MO-ODPO, Fine-Grained RLHF (arXiv 2306.01693), ArmoRM (arXiv 2406.12845), MaxMin-RLHF (arXiv 2402.08925) and [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) all do. Not one trains on a vector. Keeping components separate means *deferring the choice of aggregator* so that it can be an input at training time and a control at deployment time, and so that evaluation can report a frontier rather than a point. A Practical Guide to Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning and Planning (arXiv 2103.09568) gives the only three reasons this deferral is worth anything — the weights are unknown at learning time, the utility function is non-linear, or preferences change after deployment — and says outright that if none applies you should just write one utility function. It also supplies the distinction worth stating explicitly: applying the aggregator to each episode's realized outcome pair and then averaging is Expected Scalarised Returns, whereas applying it to the expectation is Scalarised Expected Returns, and in a stochastic environment these are different criteria with different optimal policies.

Three concrete things separation buys, each of which a reviewer may ask us to name. Evaluation moves from a point to a frontier, which is the roadmap's stated reporting rule: if objectives are combined, it is done explicitly, reporting all objectives for all solutions. Over-optimization becomes detectable and repairable — ArmoRM can decorrelate each of its nineteen named objectives from verbosity precisely because verbosity is still a separate column, and no such repair exists on a collapsed scalar. And some aggregators are unreachable by any fixed weight: max-min picks a different binding objective at every policy-iteration step (arXiv 2402.08925), while the geometric mean and weighted Tchebycheff aggregation are non-linear and reach concave regions of a front that no linear weight can touch.

### Known failure modes

**Weight-sampling mode collapse, in both directions, with a task-dependent optimum.** Conditional Language Policy reports that prompt-only conditioning "largely exhibits a mode-collapsed behavior" while its parameter-space variants give evenly spaced fronts — the headline risk of the prompt route. MO-ODPO's counter-evidence is that with the right sampling distribution prompt-only wins anyway, making the collapse a sampling problem rather than an intrinsic property. Their sweep over the Dirichlet concentration parameter found opposite failures on two corpora: on the helpful/harmless corpus, low concentration caused *bimodal* collapse (the policy could be helpful or harmless but never balanced) and the best setting was 1.0; on the summarization corpus, high concentration caused *single-point* collapse and the best setting was 0.7. Conditional Language Policy found the opposite-signed tendency on summarization, where a narrower distribution improved steerability at slight cost to the front. Practical reading: the sampling distribution is a real hyperparameter with a cheap sweep and a task-dependent optimum, and it should be swept once, early, and then frozen. One mechanism from outside the prompt-conditioning family is worth borrowing here even though its parameter-space route is not: [Janus-Protocol](../related-work/Janus-Protocol/paper_analysis.md) names the same failure as scalar collapse — the conditioning network learns to ignore its scalar and emit one average setting — and counters it with a regularizer that forces the induced configurations apart in proportion to the distance between the two scalars that produced them. The transferable idea is that conditioning collapse can be attacked directly with a term that rewards behavioral separation between distant settings, rather than only indirectly through the sampling distribution; the paper's own numbers are not usable, since every evaluation opponent is drawn from its training pool and the regularizer's weight defaults to zero in its released code.

**Variance-scale interference, which bites GRPO specifically.** MO-GRPO (arXiv 2509.22047) proves that when reward components are summed and *then* group-normalized, the advantage's correlation with component i scales as that component's standard deviation over the total standard deviation. The high-variance component dominates the gradient and the low-variance one is effectively ignored regardless of the weight assigned to it. Their fix is to standardize each component within the group before combining, which equalizes the per-objective correlation. This threatens us directly: against a frozen buyer, the buyer's realized points are likely to vary less across rollouts than the seller's own, so a naive weighted sum could make the welfare weight (λ) inert in exactly the regime the project cares about. We nonetheless defer the fix in the base setup, for a reason specific to our reward: both terms are already in the same unit (points out of the same scale), which is a luxury the single-turn papers do not have — they are gluing together incommensurable learned reward models, which is why MO-ODPO had to rescale all scores to roughly the unit interval and Rewards-in-Context had to standardize each dimension over its corpus. Standardizing our two components separately would strip the shared unit and change λ's meaning from "points" to "standard deviations of relative advantage", destroying the interpretation that makes the knob interesting. The resolution is to instrument for the failure rather than pre-empt it: log the within-group standard deviation of each side's points separately at every training step, and switch to per-component standardization the moment their ratio drifts toward zero, or the moment a third reward term on a different scale is added.

**Interpolation gaps at untrained weights.** Conditional Language Policy's central empirical result is that zero-shot parameter interpolation is sub-optimal at intermediate weightings never seen during training, and that multi-task training over sampled weightings is what fixes it; they also prove that zero-shot methods are near-Pareto-optimal only under conditions that fail in general. The corollary is that the profile range must be *trained over*, not trained at the endpoints and interpolated — and that a pair of endpoint specialists, interpolated, is the baseline that isolates multi-task training over the range as the thing that matters.

Four further cautions carry over intact. Linear scalarization can only reach the convex hull of the achievable set, so a front with a visible gap or an abrupt jump between clusters is a convexity failure rather than a training bug (arXiv 2509.11452), and the front's shape should be checked before claiming the sweep traces it. Prompt-encoding sensitivity is flagged by Conditional Language Policy and measured by nobody, which interacts badly with the rule that prompt wording stays stable across a comparison sequence: pick the encoding once, before the sequence starts. Steerability curves need correcting for response length and for divergence from the reference policy — MO-ODPO needed a full appendix for each, and an uncorrected curve is not evidence. And a front computed with the same reward models used for training is liable to be hacked, which MO-ODPO answered with an independent automated rater; our rule-verified points reduce but do not eliminate the analogous risk, which for us is a seller manipulating what the frozen buyer *reports* rather than genuinely conceding.

**The knob is not free from instruction-following.** L1/LCPO isolates the two claims that matter most here. Prior reasoning models do not follow length constraints given in prompts, and supervised fine-tuning on knob-labelled data does not learn length control either; only the reinforcement-learning stage installs a prompted numeric knob with high precision, and that knob then generalizes out of distribution and to unseen sampling parameters. This is the strongest single argument that a profile-conditioned policy needs GRPO rather than a conditioned-SFT shortcut, and it makes prompt-only conditioning of an untrained checkpoint the first baseline to run rather than an afterthought.

### The counter-position, and what its rejection teaches

[GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) made the opposite call on the axis this project is about, and defended it. It shares our two distinctive structures — a reasoning chain that states estimated utilities for both parties, and a reward mixing both parties' welfare — but fixes the per-party coefficient vectors before training on the stated grounds that pre-defined coefficients "prevent entangling welfare definitions with optimization dynamics", and it aggregates the two sides by the geometric mean rather than a linear blend, arguing zero-dominance (either side at zero collapses the reward) and diminishing returns toward the weaker side. Its steering happens at inference by halting generation at the payoff-matrix marker, editing the matrix, splicing it back and resuming — a control point *inside the trace after it is written*, where ours is *in the prompt before it is written*. The answer to their argument is available but has to be made rather than assumed: a profile that varies across episodes rather than over training keeps the training distribution of λ stationary while each episode differs.

Four operational lessons transfer from that paper regardless of the disagreement. Its reward carried an explicit anti-degeneracy constraint because the model was observed collapsing to symmetric payoff matrices, which destroys the incentive structure — a reward term that scores the *structure* of the written reasoning, not only the outcome. Its negative result is a warning about the alternative route: reinforcement learning directly on classic sequential games including Rubinstein bargaining was unstable, with reward oscillating without convergence and only marginal, non-systematic transfer, which is why their real task is assistant dialogue. Its user study found computed welfare correlated with rated satisfaction at 0.771 Pearson and 0.805 Spearman, which is the cheapest available validation that a composite welfare number tracks something a person recognizes. And its reviewer reception — rejected at ICLR 2026 with ratings 2, 2, 2, 8, with the area chair discounting one negative review as likely machine-written — turned on two demands we should pre-empt rather than argue with: an ablation isolating the structured reasoning format from the reward (train the same reward without the tagged format), and evidence at a scale above 3B with variance or confidence intervals reported. The empirical results were not disputed; the framing and the evidence base were.

### The recipe, stated concretely

Both reward terms live in the same unit, so scalarize per episode as the welfare weight times the seller's points plus one minus the welfare weight times the buyer's points, each expressed as a fraction of that side's own maximum, and add no extra scaling constant — a constant destroys λ's economic meaning. Apply GRPO's within-group centering but **do not divide by the group standard deviation**, because dividing over-amplifies low-variance groups, which at the extreme profiles is exactly where the groups will be tightest. Use **one persuasion profile per comparison group**: all rollouts in a group share the scenario *and* the profile, so the group-relative advantage answers "given this profile, which of these dialogues was better" rather than confounding good play with a favorable setting; treat the scenario-and-profile pair as the prompt. This is the opposite of RC-GRPO's within-group variation of its reward token, which is correct for its problem (restoring within-group variance under a single objective) and wrong for ours. **Sample the profile independently of the scenario**, following PERSONA (arXiv 2407.17387), whose stated reason applies verbatim: if the conditioning variable correlates with the scenario, the model learns the scenario as a proxy and the steerability claim becomes unfalsifiable. **Log both sides' points separately, always**, treating the blended reward as a derived column — this is what makes the variance-ratio diagnostic, the length and divergence corrections, and the frontier plot possible at all. Quantize the profile grid and hold out two interior values from training entirely, so that evaluating on them tests whether the model learned a continuous control or memorized a set of discrete modes.

## 3. What plays the opponent during training

Verdict: the median accepted design in this area trains against one frozen prompted counterpart, evaluates against one or two counterpart models from other families, reports per counterpart rather than averaging, and states the freezing as a deliberate scope constraint with a reason. Escalating past that is not automatically better — a pool of static frontier counterparts without co-evolutionary pressure has been measured performing *worse* than any single counterpart — and full bilateral co-evolution is a second training system rather than a switch.

**The hierarchy of rigor**, weakest to strongest. Rung zero is one frozen counterpart evaluated only against itself, which nobody credible publishes any more and which is the configuration the reviewer attack assumes. Rung one is one frozen counterpart in training plus held-out counterpart models at evaluation, with the freezing named as a deliberate scope choice — ToMAP, Strategic-Persuasion, bilateral trade and Moloch's Bargain all sit here, **and this is the median accepted design at the venues we are targeting**. Rung two adds counterpart variation inside training — personas, difficulty, or sampled preference distributions — which is where Be Selfish, But Wisely's *winning* variant sits. Rung three samples from a counterpart pool per episode. Rung four is co-evolution with held-out counterparts sharing no parameters, prompts or reward logic, plus a self-play collapse check. Rung five, human counterparts at evaluation, is orthogonal rather than above, and rung six — human-anchored self-play evaluated in a live human league — is Cicero alone and is a citation rather than a bar.

**Rung three is not automatically above rung two**, and this is the negative result that most constrains the cheap version of the fix. IB-RL trained a unilateral agent against a pool of three static frontier counterparts sampled per prompt and it scored 64.2 percent on the primary success metric — below all three single-counterpart baselines and below even the weakest of them at 78.8 percent. Their diagnosis is that gradients from counterparts requiring different strategies interfere, and that counterpart diversity without co-evolutionary pressure does not substitute for a gradually shifting opponent curriculum. The cost of escalating all the way is also measured: bilateral training without the checkpoint pool and staggered updates decays unrecoverably after roughly 25 iterations of a 90-iteration budget, sharing the reward across roles produces collusive conventions, and normalizing advantages over a joint pool corrupts the update because the roles have different reward scales. Co-evolution is a package, not a switch. Two calibration facts belong in any rebuttal: ToMAP is well cited, has public code, reports a held-out counterpart on which its method loses to the untrained base model, and was accepted anyway — reviewers accept partial transfer when it is measured and reject transfer when it is merely asserted; and the frozen-counterpart choice has a defensible technical rationale already in print three times over (a stationary reward landscape, no mode collapse from co-adaptation, and a counterpart of sufficient quality).

**The adopted protocol.** Keep one frozen prompted buyer as the base of the environment and turn it from a fixed policy into a distribution, along three axes in priority order. First, a walk-away-capable buyer with a real outside option — not optional, and the highest-value item on the list, because if the buyer accepts anything then the proself end of the profile range is measured against a counterpart that cannot punish selfishness and the whole frontier is an artifact. Second, buyer persona and concession-style variation sampled per episode on top of the hidden priority draw, which is the cheapest rung-two move, costs one prompt-template field, and is exactly what beat reward shaping in Be Selfish, But Wisely. Third, and only after the first two are in place, multiple buyer base models sampled per episode, flagged as the risky axis given the pool result above; if we do it, prefer models from one family at different sizes and check the training return per counterpart. Evaluation runs three tiers, all held out, all reported per tier and never averaged into one number: in-distribution generalization (same buyer model, unseen priority draws and personas), counterpart shift (two or three buyer models from other families, each reported separately across the full profile sweep), and adversarial plus self-play diagnostics (a hostile buyer prompt, plus placing the trained seller in the buyer seat against itself and comparing to its in-system score).

**Escalation triggers, measured before escalating.** Run the counterpart-shift and self-play tiers once the first steerable checkpoint exists, before scaling anything, and escalate only if one of three fires. A transfer gap: seller points or the achieved frontier degrades materially on a held-out buyer — specifically, if the profile-to-behavior mapping inverts or flattens. A modest uniform drop is normal and reportable; a *shape* change in the frontier is disqualifying, because the frontier is the claim. A self-play collapse: an in-system-minus-self-play gap that looks like the unilateral policies' 30-to-40-point drop rather than a small one. Visible degeneracy: a fixed script that works only on this buyer, a rising rate of buyer acceptances below its own walk-away during training, or opponent-estimate accuracy that stays high on the training buyer and collapses off it. If none fires, write the scope sentence and spend the compute on the profile sweep.

**The structural defense**, which is stronger than any of the above and specific to this design: the reward already contains the buyer's points at every profile below the proself extreme, and that term is computed from the buyer's *ground-truth hidden priorities*, not from the buyer's behavior. A seller that discovers a quirk of this particular frozen buyer gains nothing on that term. The same holds for the opponent-estimate score, which is graded against the true priority order, so counterpart-specific verbal shortcuts cannot inflate it. Two of the three headline quantities are therefore immune to the failure the attack describes; the transfer experiments are corroboration, not the argument. Cheap mitigations to install regardless: a divergence penalty to the supervised reference during GRPO, cited as the direct descendant of the interleaved supervised updates and human anchors above rather than as a hyperparameter; buyer-side sanity metrics logged throughout training (buyer points, buyer walk-away rate, rate of buyer acceptances below its walk-away) so the frozen-partner pathology appears in the training curves instead of at evaluation; and the in-system versus held-out divergence plot, which turns "are we memorizing the buyer" into a picture.

## 4. Measurement instruments

Verdict: the measurement side is where this setting has a real structural advantage, because the two headline columns and the mechanism probe are all arithmetic on ground truth that the environment generated. The design rule that follows is that every number in a report should be producible by a cell reading persisted sources with no judge in the loop, which is achievable for the core panel and is not achievable for the honesty panel — hence the deferral in the last subsection.

**Efficiency and surplus share are the two outcome columns, and both have precedent.** Efficiency (joint points achieved over the maximum joint points available) is the axiom check; surplus share (points above walk-away over the joint surplus above both walk-aways) is what the persuasion profile steers. CaSiNo (arXiv 2103.15721) supplies the human anchor for the first: participants do find the joint gains, with a correlation of 0.425 between the structural integrative potential of a scenario pair and the joint points achieved, and the allocation space is small enough to enumerate, so Pareto-optimality of a deal is a free exact check rather than an estimate. Bilateral trade (arXiv 2604.16472) supplies the precedent for the second and the reason the two columns must be reported together: SFT there raised surplus share by 15 to 40 points while dropping deal rates, reinforcement learning recovered deal rates while eroding surplus back toward the base model, and the full pipeline landed within about two points of the base model on most metrics — a tension traced to a reward that assigns zero utility to rational walk-aways and irrational acceptances alike. Multi-buyer bargaining (arXiv 2607.05863) reports the same quantity as a surplus-extraction ratio. The operational lesson is that a composite reward must distinguish a rational walk-away from a failed negotiation, and that deal rate and walk-away rate belong beside the two headline columns rather than in an appendix. [Negotiation-Breakdown](../related-work/Negotiation-Breakdown/paper_analysis.md) is the precedent for treating breakdown as a first-class labelled outcome with its own detector, and its corpus statistics are a useful reminder that a high agreement rate and a high rate of good agreements are different things — its harder corpus reaches 92.9 percent agreement with only 0.98 percent of bids Pareto-optimal.

**Opponent estimates in ranking form, and how to score them.** With three issues and a total priority order there are six possible orderings, which makes the estimate a small discrete object and gives four scoring options that cost nothing once the trace is parsed: exact-match accuracy over the six orderings; pairwise ranking accuracy over the three pairs, which is the form the standard task uses; rank correlation between the stated order and the true one, which grades partial credit smoothly; and, if the trace states an explicit distribution rather than a point estimate, the Brier score (mean squared error between stated probabilities and the one-hot truth) against a uniform reference. Two ready-made templates exist. Opponent Modeling in Negotiation Dialogues by Related Data Adaptation (arXiv 2205.00344) defines the task as inferring the opponent's priority order from a partial dialogue, frames it as pairwise ranking, evaluates on CaSiNo and Deal or No Deal at varying amounts of dialogue observed, and releases code — this is the cleanest existing definition of mid-dialogue partner-preference accuracy, and its "accuracy as a function of dialogue fraction" reporting shape is the right one. BOND (arXiv 2605.04507) is closer still: a posterior over the six orderings, updated each turn, distilled into an 8B student that emits the belief as tagged text, scored by Brier against the true ordering (teacher 0.085, distilled student 0.114, uniform reference 0.139, and a prompted structured-reasoning baseline's elicited posterior at 0.194, worse than uniform), with a belief-versus-policy error decomposition separating "wrong belief" from "right belief, wrong action". Its headline negative finding — belief-action coupling remains limited after supervised distillation, so the student reports a good belief and then fails to act on it — is the published opening this project's reinforcement-learning stage is aimed at.

**The oracle-versus-inferred design** prices what estimation costs, and its precedent is the paper that also justifies probing at all. Large Language Models Persuade Without Planning Theory of Mind (arXiv 2602.17045) constructs matched conditions in which the target's mental states are either given or must be inferred; models fail in the hidden condition while still out-persuading humans in realistic scenarios, which the authors attribute to rhetorical strategy rather than mental-state modeling. Persuasion success and opponent modeling come apart empirically, which is why opponent modeling must be measured directly rather than read off task reward. Running the same scenarios with the buyer's true priorities handed to the seller, and again with them hidden, gives the gap that the estimate is worth. One caution attaches: SToM (arXiv 2510.18476) reports a belief-driven agent *outperforming* an agent given full access to the partner's intentions, so the oracle condition should be reported as a reference point rather than assumed to be an upper bound.

**The trace-corruption intervention** is the causal test that separates a trace the policy uses from a trace the policy merely writes, and the mechanism for it already exists. [GTAlign](../related-work/GTAlign/paper_analysis.md) demonstrates halting generation at a tag boundary, editing the numeric block, splicing it back and resuming, using it as a steering control. Used as a test rather than as a control, the same splice answers BOND's question mechanically: overwrite the stated opponent estimate with a corrupted ordering, resume generation, and measure how much the chosen offer moves. A policy whose action is invariant to the corruption is one whose estimate is decorative; the size of the shift is the coupling measurement, and it is available without any new model or annotation. Note that GTAlign's own limitations section flags that abrupt payoff edits may destabilize responses across turns, so the intervention should be applied at a fixed point in the trace and reported with the resulting deal-rate change.

**The before-and-after regression** is the steerability instrument, and it comes from [Chatbot-Ads](../related-work/Chatbot-Ads/paper_analysis.md) (arXiv 2604.08525). Their logistic model decomposes a choice under conflicting incentives into three terms: a base propensity to favor the principal's interest, a sensitivity to user utility, and a sensitivity to the principal's utility, with the utilities expressed in the setting's own units. Fitting it before and after training turns "did training change the welfare trade-off" into three numbers rather than a picture. The critical methodological detail — and the reason this instrument is a check rather than a restatement of our claim — is that they tried the constrained one-parameter version, a single weight mixing user and company utility, and it fit *worse* by McFadden's pseudo-R-squared and yielded weights outside the unit interval for some models, which is why they use the two-parameter form. That is the published evidence that a single coherent welfare weight cannot be fitted to untrained model behavior, and it is the falsifier-facing baseline this project's claim rests against. Two of their measurement design choices transfer directly: the soft-instruction framing (an encouragement rather than a command) isolates a model's default disposition from its instruction-following, which is what a clean pre-training baseline condition needs; and their persona-differential methodology, contrasting implicit contextual cues against explicit stated attributes with affordability stress tests, is the template for asking whether a trained seller modulates tactics by counterpart vulnerability. Their reviewers' unanswered criticisms are worth inheriting as design constraints rather than repeating: a single domain, a maximally extreme conflict design with no intermediate difficulty levels, an unvalidated judge, and no non-moral instruction-following control.

**The reasoning-trace precedent line, and the composition verdict.** Explicit lookahead in dialogue agents has five recognizable ancestors, and each is separated from this design on a different axis. Strategic Reasoning with Language Models (arXiv 2305.19165, Gandhi et al.) is the closest match on trace *format* — value estimates over candidate actions written out in visible reasoning, with beliefs about the other party, generalizing to new game structures and hidden information — but nothing is trained and the estimates are never scored, so generalization is measured only by task outcome. GDP-Zero (arXiv 2305.13660) runs open-loop tree search where a single prompted model plays policy prior, value function, user simulator and system model at once; the value estimates are tree statistics, never text, and the objective is one-sided. PPDPP (arXiv 2311.00262) trains a small planner in front of a frozen large model with supervised strategy labels then reinforcement learning from goal-oriented feedback, but the planner emits a discrete strategy label rather than a numeric estimate. Cicero separates strictly: every expected value is computed in a neural planner and only the chosen plan crosses into language, and the dialogue model is never trained on game reward — its follow-up (arXiv 2502.12436) reads three counterfactual utility quantities off the value function to detect deception mechanically at 0.950 precision against human labels versus 0.161 F-score for direct language-model judging, which is the precedent for "score a structured utility quantity instead of asking a judge", though as an external detector rather than the agent grading itself. BOND, as above, has candidate enumeration, both parties' utilities, a trade-off coefficient and a mechanical ground-truth probe in this exact game, but trains by supervised distillation, computes the both-party numbers in an external symbolic solver so they never appear in the student's text, and explicitly disowns its coefficient as a steering control. **The composition verdict**: each ingredient is individually precedented — value estimates in a visible trace (prompted only), enumerate-candidates-and-score-both-sides (external solver only), reinforcement learning on multi-turn negotiation (no probe), and mechanical scoring of stated beliefs against hidden ground truth (a posterior, by Brier score) — and the composition is what is unoccupied. The claim worth defending is narrower and cleaner than "we do foresight": the utility estimates in the reasoning trace are simultaneously the mechanism and the measurement instrument, so the opponent-modeling probe is free because the trace already contains the numbers.

**Deferred instruments: the honesty panel.** A three-part honesty panel is designed and deliberately out of scope for the first paper. Its first component is a mechanical self-claim check: extract structured claims about the seller's own priorities from each turn and evaluate each in code against the assigned order, reporting the false rate over checkable claims together with the raw claim count and the checkable share. This is available because the seller's own priorities are in its prompt, which removes the expensive half of every published honesty benchmark — the MASK benchmark (arXiv 2503.03750) spends five probes per proposition establishing what a model believes before it can call a statement a lie, and this environment's lie label is definitional rather than inferred. Its second component is belief misalignment: elicit the buyer's point estimate of the seller's priorities before and after the dialogue, take the change in distance to the truth, with copyable released code from Evaluating and Reducing Deceptive Dialogue From Language Models with Multi-turn RL (arXiv 2510.14318), whose own result that penalizing this quantity in the reward cut deception by 77.6 percent is also the warning that it must not be both a training signal and a headline metric. Its third component is the paltering signature: the reason the panel needs all three parts is that every study measuring the axes separately found the withholding-and-paltering axis dominant and outright falsification small — AI-LieDar (arXiv 2409.09013) reports partial lies at 38.9 to 75.5 percent against falsification at 6.2 to 30.3 percent across six models, and Used Car Salesbots (arXiv 2605.31445) finds informed agents clustered at "misleading or withheld" rather than lying. A mechanical lie rate reported alone would very likely be a flat line, and a *falling checkable share with a flat false rate* is the signature of a policy that learned to palter, which is the most interesting result the instrument could produce. Two hygiene rules from that literature apply to the core panel too, not only to the deferred one: whatever extracts a claim from text is doing the measurement and needs its own validation number reported alongside every result — a decoder ablation elsewhere flipped the same data from a strong finding to near-noise (arXiv 2606.01456) — and any judge model and prompt must be frozen before a comparison sweep begins, since model-based social evaluation is known to overestimate relative to human judgment and to drift with model version.

## Implications for this project

The control channel is forced rather than chosen, and that is the sentence to write: reasoning-visible objective control admits exactly one mechanism, and that constraint is what makes the opponent-estimate probe possible at all. Two adjacent ideas belong on the backlog rather than in the design — a profile-conditioned steering vector as an auxiliary amplifier if the prompted control turns out weak at the extremes, and extracting a prosociality direction from the trained checkpoint to test whether its projection tracks the prompted welfare weight, which would be an internal-validity check on the control that nothing in the taxonomy has run.

Six things change what we build. The per-episode scalar reward keeps both terms in the shared points unit with no extra scaling constant, and the group update centers without dividing by the group standard deviation. One persuasion profile per comparison group, with the scenario-and-profile pair treated as the prompt. The profile is sampled independently of the scenario, and two interior values are held out of training to test whether the control is continuous or memorized. Both sides' points are logged separately at every step, along with their within-group standard deviations, so that the variance-interference failure appears as a drifting ratio rather than as an inert control discovered at evaluation. The prompt encoding of the profile is frozen before the first comparison run. And the steerability curve is reported with the length and reference-divergence corrections that MO-ODPO needed, because an uncorrected curve is not evidence.

Three baselines each kill a specific alternative explanation and should be run before anything expensive: prompt-only profiles on the untrained instruct checkpoint, which L1/LCPO's appendices predict will fail to track the control and which, if it succeeds, shrinks the contribution dramatically; conditioned SFT on profile-labelled trajectories, which the same paper predicts is also insufficient; and two endpoint specialists interpolated in weight space, which Conditional Language Policy predicts is fine at the endpoints and sub-optimal in between, isolating multi-task training over the range as the thing that matters.

On the opponent, we sit at rung one deliberately and say so, with the walk-away-capable buyer and per-episode persona variation moving us to rung two at the cost of one prompt field. We measure before escalating, on the three named triggers, and we lead the rebuttal with the structural defense rather than with the transfer experiments: two of the three headline quantities are scored against the buyer's ground-truth hidden priorities rather than against buyer behavior, so counterpart-specific exploitation cannot inflate them.

On measurement, the core panel is efficiency, surplus share, opponent-estimate accuracy in ranking form, and deal-and-walk-away rates, every one of them arithmetic on persisted files with no judge in the loop. Three instruments extend it at low cost and high evidential value: the oracle-versus-inferred gap, reported as a reference point rather than a ceiling; the trace-corruption splice, which converts BOND's belief-action-coupling gap from a limitation we cite into a number we report; and the before-and-after regression, whose own two-parameter-beats-one-parameter result is the published statement that a single coherent welfare weight does not fit untrained behavior. The honesty panel stays designed and unbuilt, named in the paper as scope rather than omitted, because the instrument most likely to show nothing is the one that costs least and the two that would show something are the two that need a validated extractor and a frozen judge.

Two anticipations, taken from how the nearest paper was received. Expect to be asked for the ablation that trains the same reward without the structured reasoning format, and for evidence above 3B with dispersion reported — both were the grounds on which GTAlign's mechanism was accepted while its framing was rejected. And expect the reward to be the place where the surplus-versus-deal-rate tension surfaces, since bilateral trade hit it accidentally with a reward that could not distinguish a rational walk-away from a failure; here the welfare weight is a principled handle on exactly that tension, which is the motivating contrast worth stating up front.
